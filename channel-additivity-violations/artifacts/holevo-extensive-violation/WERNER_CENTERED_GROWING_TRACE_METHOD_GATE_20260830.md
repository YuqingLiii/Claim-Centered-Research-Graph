# Werner centered bound: growing-order trace-method gate

Date: 2026-08-30

Status: proposer-side analytic note for `HEXT.same-tuple-werner-centered-bound`.  No canonical
YAML is changed.  The high-probability centered bound remains mathematically unresolved; its
canonical node stays `CONJECTURED`.  None of the proposed DAG changes below should be promoted
beyond `CONJECTURED` without independent review.

No numerical experiment is used in this note.

## 1. Setup and verdict

Let `U_1,...,U_m` be independent Haar unitaries in `U(d)` and put

\[
 R_U=\frac1{m^2}\sum_{v,a=1}^m
 U_v^*U_a\otimes U_a^*U_v,
 \qquad
 S_U=R_U-I/m.
\tag{1}
\]

Since the `v=a` terms in `R_U` sum to `I/m`, if

\[
 E_m:=\{(v,a):1\le v,a\le m,\ v\ne a\},\qquad
 A_{v,a}:=U_v^*U_a,
\tag{2}
\]

then

\[
 S_U=\frac1{m^2}\sum_{e\in E_m}A_e\otimes A_e^*.
\tag{3}
\]

The Werner target is

\[
 \Pr\{\|S_U\|_\infty>h_W\}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad
 h_W:=\sqrt{(A^2-1)(1-m^{-2})},
 \quad
 A=1+\frac{9(1+\eta)^2}{m}.
\tag{4}
\]

For fixed `eta` and large `m`,

\[
 h_W=\frac{\sqrt{18}(1+\eta)}{\sqrt m}
 \left(1+O\left(\frac{(1+\eta)^2}{m}\right)\right).
\tag{5}
\]

The outcome of the trace-method attack is sharply delimited:

1. There is an exact block-word formula for every even trace moment.  Its second trace contains
   the **reverse order** of the first block word.  Except at order two, it is not a modulus square.
2. All terms for which either block word reduces to the identity admit a rigorous bound with
   spectral constant at most `(3m-4)/m^2 ~ 3/m`.  This is comfortably below the required
   `sqrt(18)/sqrt(m)` scale.  Return words are not the obstruction.
3. Replacing the reverse correlation by a modulus-square majorant loses the ordinary
   expectation-plus-Markov route.  The majorant has an explicit annular/invariant-contraction
   expectation floor at every order.  A distinguished singleton-letter family proves that its
   Markov bound is vacuous at the target scale.
4. Fixed-order second-order freeness says that the exact nonreturn moment is supported at leading
   order only on block words whose free reductions are cyclically equivalent after block
   reversal.  However, the theorem is not uniform for `k=Theta(log d)`.
5. The unresolved object is therefore a joint **reduction-plus-reversal collision count**, along
   with a growing-degree Weingarten estimate having only exponential, not factorial, diagram
   loss.  Naive absolute Weingarten enumeration produces a factor of the form `(Ck)^{Ck}` and is
   quantitatively unusable at `k=c log d`.

Thus the growing trace route is not closed here, but its safe component, its exact interface loss,
and the smallest next lemma are identified.  Nothing below is a Haar-probability counterexample
to (4).

## 2. Exact trace expansion: reversal, not a square

Fix an even integer `n=2k`.  For

\[
 \mathbf e=(e_1,\ldots,e_n)\in E_m^n
\tag{6}
\]

define the forward and block-reversed matrix words

\[
 W_U(\mathbf e):=A_{e_1}\cdots A_{e_n},
 \qquad
 \check W_U(\mathbf e):=A_{e_n}\cdots A_{e_1}.
\tag{7}
\]

Expanding (3) and taking the trace on both `d`-dimensional tensor factors gives exactly

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{Tr}_{d^2}(S_U^n)
 =\frac1{m^{2n}}
 \sum_{\mathbf e\in E_m^n}
 \operatorname{Tr}_d W_U(\mathbf e)\,
 \overline{\operatorname{Tr}_d\check W_U(\mathbf e)}.}
\tag{8}
\]

Indeed,

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(A_{e_1}^*\cdots A_{e_n}^*)
 =\overline{\operatorname{Tr}(A_{e_n}\cdots A_{e_1})}.
\tag{9}
\]

For `n=2`, cyclicity of trace identifies the two words, and (8) is a sum of squares.  For
`n>=4`, cyclicity does **not** reverse the order, so in general

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(A_1\cdots A_n)
 \overline{\operatorname{Tr}(A_n\cdots A_1)}
 \ne |\operatorname{Tr}(A_1\cdots A_n)|^2.
\tag{10}
\]

This distinction is load-bearing.  Treating (8) as a square would erase precisely the
reverse-word cancellation that a successful growing-moment proof must retain.

For bookkeeping, let `F_m` be the free group generated by `g_1,...,g_m`, set

\[
 x_{v,a}:=g_v^{-1}g_a,
\tag{11}
\]

and let `w(\mathbf e)` and `\check w(\mathbf e)` be the corresponding forward and block-reversed
free-group products.  If

\[
 C_n(g,h):=\#\{\mathbf e\in E_m^n:
 w(\mathbf e)=g,\ \check w(\mathbf e)=h\},
\tag{12}
\]

then (8) also gives the exact expectation identity

\[
 \mathbb E\operatorname{Tr}(S_U^n)
 =\frac1{m^{2n}}\sum_{g,h\in F_m}C_n(g,h)K_d(g,h),
 \qquad
 K_d(g,h):=\mathbb E\big[\operatorname{Tr}U_g\,
 \overline{\operatorname{Tr}U_h}\big].
\tag{13}
\]

Equation (13), rather than a one-word return count, is the exact combinatorial object.

## 3. The return sector is rigorously safe

Let `lambda` denote the left-regular representation of `F_m`, and define the self-adjoint group
algebra element

\[
 T_m:=\sum_{v\ne a}\lambda(g_v^{-1}g_a).
\tag{14}
\]

The number of length-`n` edge sequences whose **forward** word reduces to the identity is

\[
 N_{\rm ret}(m,n)=\tau(T_m^n).
\tag{15}
\]

Writing `X_m=sum_v lambda(g_v)`,

\[
 T_m=X_m^*X_m-mI.
\tag{16}
\]

The standard Akemann--Ostrand/Kesten norm formula

\[
 \|X_m\|=2\sqrt{m-1}\qquad(m\ge2)
\tag{17}
\]

implies

\[
 \|T_m\|\le 3m-4.
\tag{18}
\]

For the present conclusion, even the homogeneous length-two Haagerup inequality
`||T_m||<=3sqrt(m(m-1))` would suffice.  Since `n` is even, (15)--(18) yield

\[
 N_{\rm ret}(m,n)\le(3m-4)^n.
\tag{19}
\]

Block reversal is a bijection on `E_m^n`, so the same count holds for `\check w=1`.  Every summand
in (8) has magnitude at most `d^2`.  Consequently the total absolute contribution of all sequences
for which `w=1` or `\check w=1` is at most

\[
 2d^2\left(\frac{3m-4}{m^2}\right)^n.
\tag{20}
\]

At the Werner threshold, its base relative to `h_W` is

\[
 \rho_{\rm ret}:=\frac{3m-4}{m^2h_W}
 =\frac1{\sqrt{2m}(1+\eta)}
 \left(1+O\left(\frac{(1+\eta)^2}{m}\right)\right).
\tag{21}
\]

Thus `2d^2 rho_ret^{2k}` tends to zero after choosing

\[
 k>\frac{(1+\delta)\log d}{-\log\rho_{\rm ret}}
 =\left(2+o_m(1)\right)\frac{(1+\delta)\log d}{\log m}.
\tag{22}
\]

This proves a useful negative diagnosis: neither identity returns nor the free spectral constant
forces the problematic `sqrt(18)/sqrt(m)` constant.  They have an extra factor `m^{-1/2}` of
room.

As a concrete check, sequences satisfying

\[
 e_{n+1-j}=e_j^{-1}\quad(1\le j\le k)
\tag{23}
\]

make both block words the identity.  There are exactly `|E_m|^k=[m(m-1)]^k` such sequences, and
their deterministic contribution is

\[
 d^2\left(\frac{m-1}{m^3}\right)^k.
\tag{24}
\]

Relative to `h_W^{2k}~(18(1+eta)^2/m)^k`, (24) has base approximately
`1/(18(1+eta)^2m)`, again safely below one.

## 4. A rigorous obstruction to modulus-square majorization

Because `S_U` is Hermitian, `Tr(S_U^n)>=0`.  Applying Cauchy--Schwarz to (8) and using the fact
that block reversal permutes `E_m^n` gives the pointwise upper bound

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(S_U^n)\le Q_n(U),
 \qquad
 Q_n(U):=\frac1{m^{2n}}
 \sum_{\mathbf e\in E_m^n}|\operatorname{Tr}W_U(\mathbf e)|^2.
\tag{25}
\]

This is a valid upper bound, but it is too lossy for (4).  The failure can be proved exactly.

Fix the first edge to have tail label `1`, and require label `1` to occur in no other one of the
`2n-1` vertex slots.  There are

\[
 N_{\rm sing}(m,n)
 =(m-1)[(m-1)(m-2)]^{n-1}
 =(m-1)^n(m-2)^{n-1}
\tag{26}
\]

such edge sequences.  Conditional on `U_2,...,U_m`, every corresponding forward word has the
form `U_1^*V` for a fixed unitary `V`; hence it is Haar and

\[
 \mathbb E_{U_1}|\operatorname{Tr}(U_1^*V)|^2=1.
\tag{27}
\]

All summands defining `Q_n` are nonnegative, so if

\[
 q_m:=\frac{(m-1)(m-2)}{m^2},
\tag{28}
\]

then for every `n>=1` and `m>=3`,

\[
 \boxed{\mathbb E Q_n(U)\ge\frac{q_m^n}{m-2}.}
\tag{29}
\]

For `n=2k`, attempting Markov through (25) gives

\[
 \Pr\{\|S_U\|\ge h_W\}
 \le h_W^{-2k}\mathbb E\operatorname{Tr}(S_U^{2k})
 \le h_W^{-2k}\mathbb E Q_{2k}(U).
\tag{30}
\]

But the last candidate upper bound has the rigorous lower floor

\[
 h_W^{-2k}\mathbb E Q_{2k}(U)
 \ge\frac1{m-2}\left(\frac{q_m}{h_W}\right)^{2k}
 =\frac{m^{k-1}}{[18(1+\eta)^2]^k}(1+o_m(1))
\tag{31}
\]

for fixed `eta`.  In the intended large-`m` regime this is already much larger than one for
`k>=2`, and it becomes worse as `k` grows.  Thus **the ordinary positive-moment Markov route whose
final bound is `h_W^{-2k} E Q_{2k}` cannot prove the target**, regardless of how sharply that
expectation is evaluated.  This does not rule out a separately justified truncation or a direct
tail estimate for `Q_{2k}`.

The same loss can be seen representation-theoretically.  For any unitary `V`,

\[
 |\operatorname{Tr}V|^2=1+\chi_{\rm ad}(V),
\tag{32}
\]

where the `1` is the invariant summand of `V tensor bar(V)` and `chi_ad` is the character on
traceless matrices.  Therefore

\[
 Q_n(U)=\left(1-\frac1m\right)^n
 +\frac1{m^{2n}}\sum_{\mathbf e\in E_m^n}
 \chi_{\rm ad}(W_U(\mathbf e)).
\tag{33}
\]

The first term in (33) is the universal annular/invariant contraction.  A wordwise absolute-value
bound destroys the cancellation against the adjoint-character remainder.  Equation (29) shows
that this is not merely a formal decomposition: a large explicit family retains unit expectation.

Crucially, (29)--(33) obstruct the **majorant**, not the exact moment.  For `n>=4`, the singleton
terms in (8) contain a reverse-word cross-correlation and need not equal the positive quantities
in (27).  Hence this section is neither a lower bound on `E Tr(S_U^{2k})` nor a counterexample to
the desired high-probability norm bound.  At `n=2` only, block reversal is cyclic and `Q_2` equals
the exact trace moment.

## 5. What fixed-order second-order freeness actually says

For fixed reduced nontrivial free-group words `g,h`, second-order freeness for independent Haar
unitaries describes

\[
 K_d(g,h)=\mathbb E[\operatorname{Tr}U_g\,
 \overline{\operatorname{Tr}U_h}].
\tag{34}
\]

After cyclic reduction, its `d->infinity` leading term is zero unless `g` and `h` have matching
primitive cyclic words (with the appropriate powers); when they match, the limit is the number
of cyclic matches.  In particular it is at most the word length for a fixed pair.  A primary
reference is Mingo--Sniady--Speicher, *Second Order Freeness and Fluctuations of Random Matrices
II: Unitary Random Matrices*, arXiv:math/0405258.

Applied to (13), this identifies the nonreturn leading diagrams as

\[
 \operatorname{red}w(\mathbf e)
 \sim_{\rm cyc}
 \operatorname{red}\check w(\mathbf e),
\tag{35}
\]

possibly with a common primitive power.  These are the **reduction-plus-reversal collisions**.
They replace the unrestricted annular background created by (25).

Two easy subclasses are harmless:

- Literal block palindromes `mathbf e=checkmathbf e` number exactly `|E_m|^k`.  Even bounding
  each associated term by `d^2`, their total normalized size is (24).
- Allowing a cyclic shift before literal block reversal gives at most
  `2k |E_m|^{k+1}` sequences (the extra power safely covers reflections with fixed blocks).  The
  resulting crude `d^2` bound still has base `O(1/m^2)` per pair of moments, below the Werner
  base `Theta(1/m)`.

The hard set in (35) is larger because many different edge sequences reduce to the same cyclic
word.  Cancellations arise whenever adjacent blocks contain

\[
 (g_v^{-1}g_a)(g_a^{-1}g_b)=g_v^{-1}g_b,
\tag{36}
\]

and nested repetitions generate joint collision diagrams.  Counting only literal palindromes
misses these diagrams.

The cited second-order theorem has the quantifiers

\[
 \text{first fix the finite words (hence fix `k`), then let `d->infinity`.}
\tag{37}
\]

It does not give a remainder uniform for `k=c log d`.  Therefore it cannot, by itself, be inserted
into (13) to prove (4).

## 6. Why naive growing-degree Weingarten bounds also fail

A direct Haar integration of (13) introduces permutations pairing every occurrence of each
`U_v` with an occurrence of `U_v^*`.  At total degree `O(k)`, a termwise absolute enumeration has
factorial diagram count.  Even an optimistic remainder of the form

\[
 \frac{(Ck)^{Ck}}{d^2}
\tag{38}
\]

is not uniform at logarithmic order: for `k=c log d`,

\[
 \frac{(Ck)^{Ck}}{d^2}
 =\exp\{Cc(\log d)(\log\log d+O(1))-2\log d\},
\tag{39}
\]

which eventually grows for every fixed `c>0`.  On the moment scale, a bound with an effective
root `Ck/m` is likewise useless, because `k` eventually exceeds the fixed target root
`sqrt(18/m)` when `d->infinity` at fixed `m`.

This is the precise growing-order dimension loss: independent absolute summation over Weingarten
permutations forgets the genus/noncrossing cancellations that must reduce the number of relevant
shapes from factorial to exponential.  The problematic combinatorial family is not the identity
return family from Section 3.  It is the repeated-label reduction family in (36), together with
annular pairings that make the two reduced block orders in (35) cyclically coincide.

No estimate in this note proves that those diagrams are genuinely too large.  It proves only that
the two standard relaxations--modulus-square symmetrization and factorial absolute Weingarten
enumeration--cannot certify that they are small enough.

## 7. Smallest sufficient growing-order lemma

A directly usable trace lemma would be the following.  Constants may depend on fixed `eta`, but
not on `d` or `k`.

> **Centered reversal-moment lemma (missing).**  For fixed `m` in the intended regime, there are
> `c_m,delta>0` and `C_0` with
> `C_0/m<h_W` such that, for every integer
> `1<=k<=c_m log d`,
> \[
>  \mathbb E\operatorname{Tr}(S_U^{2k})
>  \le d^2\left(\frac{C_0}{m}\right)^{2k}
>     +d^{-\delta}h_W^{2k}.
> \tag{40}
> \]

The constant margin is generous:

\[
 \frac{C_0/m}{h_W}
 =\frac{C_0}{\sqrt{18}(1+\eta)\sqrt m}(1+o_m(1)).
\tag{41}
\]

Choosing

\[
 k>\frac{(1+\epsilon)\log d}
 {\log(mh_W/C_0)}
\tag{42}
\]

then makes Markov's inequality tend to zero.  Section 3 establishes the analogue of (40) for all
terms with a return word with `C_0=3+o_m(1)`.  What remains is precisely the nonreturn joint kernel
in (13).

A more atomic combinatorial/Weingarten version sufficient for (40) would prove both:

1. the reduction-plus-reversal collision diagrams have at most `C_1^k m^{2k+O(1)}` labelings,
   with `C_1` independent of `k` (not `(Ck)^{Ck}`); and
2. all positive-genus remainders admit a uniform `d^{-2g}` sum with only `C_2^k` diagram growth
   through `k<=c_m log d`.

The first bound would normalize to an `O(C_1/m^2)^k` moment scale, which is strictly smaller than
`h_W^{2k}=Theta(18/m)^k` for the very large `m` used by the project.  The second bound would allow
`c_m` to be chosen small enough to absorb the residual dimension factor.

This is the next lemma to attack.  It must retain the signed/reversal correlation in (8), or use a
nonbacktracking/centered replica polynomial that removes the invariant contraction before taking
absolute values.  The ordinary positive majorant `Q_n` cannot serve this role.

## 8. Expectation, typical norm, and deterministic statements

The logical scopes are different:

- Equations (8), (13), (19), (20), (25), (26), and (29) are exact finite-`d` or deterministic
  statements.
- Equation (34)--(35) is a fixed-word, fixed-`k` asymptotic interface.  It is not a logarithmic
  moment theorem.
- The lower bound (29) concerns `E Q_n`, not `E Tr(S_U^n)` for `n>=4` and not a typical operator
  norm.
- The all-equal tuple still gives a deterministic failure outside generic Haar probability, but
  nothing here upgrades it to a positive-probability obstruction.
- No high-probability estimate for `||S_U||` follows from this note.  The node
  `HEXT.same-tuple-werner-centered-bound` remains open at its current proposer-side status.
