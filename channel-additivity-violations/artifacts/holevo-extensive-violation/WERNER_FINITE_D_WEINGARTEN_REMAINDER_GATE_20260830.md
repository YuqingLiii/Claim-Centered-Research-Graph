# Werner reversal moments: finite-\(d\) Weingarten remainder gate

Date: 2026-08-30

Status: proposer-side analytic artifact for `HEXT.same-tuple-werner-centered-bound`. No canonical
YAML is changed. This note proves a uniform summability bound for the monotone-Weingarten excess
paths attached to any fixed contraction skeleton, and it gives an explicit repeated-label
obstruction to the stronger coefficientwise target `C^N d^{-2g}` with `C` independent of `g`.
It does not bound all nonplanar contraction skeletons, so the centered spectrum claim remains
unresolved and its canonical node remains `CONJECTURED`.

No numerical computation is used.

## 1. Outcome

Let `N=2k` be the trace-moment order. For one edge sequence, Haar integration contains one unitary
Weingarten function for each active label. If label `v` occurs with balanced degree `p_v`, then

\[
 \sum_v p_v=2N,\qquad 0\le p_v\le2N.
\tag{1}
\]

For a permutation `pi in S_p`, write `ell(pi)=p-#cycles(pi)`. The monotone-path expansion is

\[
 (-1)^{\ell(\pi)}d^{p+\ell(\pi)}
 \operatorname{Wg}_d(\pi)
 =
 \sum_{g\ge0}P_g(\pi)d^{-2g},
\tag{2}
\]

where `P_g(pi)` is the number of monotone transposition paths of length `ell(pi)+2g`.
Collins--Matsumoto prove

\[
 (p-1)^gP_0(\pi)
 \le P_g(\pi)
 \le (6p^{7/2})^gP_0(\pi).
\tag{3}
\]

This has two consequences.

1. The requested coefficientwise estimate

   \[
    \text{genus-}g\text{ coefficient}\le C^N
   \tag{4}
   \]

   with a universal `C` independent of `g` is false already for one Weingarten factor. For
   `pi=id_p`, explicit paired backtracks give

   \[
    P_g(id_p)\ge(p-1)^g.
   \tag{5}
   \]

   The repeated-edge sequence `e_1=...=e_N=(1,2)` realizes `p_1=p_2=N`, so this is an actual
   repeated-label family inside the Werner trace expansion, not an unrelated Weingarten example.

2. The failure of (4) is harmless after the factor `d^{-2g}` is retained. For an arbitrary
   multi-label contraction skeleton, the total excess-genus coefficient satisfies

   \[
    \frac{\text{excess-genus }g\text{ absolute weight}}
         {\text{minimal-path absolute weight}}
    \le [6(2N)^{7/2}]^g.
   \tag{6}
   \]

   Hence, with

   \[
    \theta_{N,d}:=\frac{6(2N)^{7/2}}{d^2},
   \tag{7}
   \]

   all positive monotone-path excesses sum to at most

   \[
    \frac{\theta_{N,d}}{1-\theta_{N,d}}
   \tag{8}
   \]

   times the absolute minimal-path skeleton weight whenever `theta_{N,d}<1`.

For `N<=c_m log d`,

\[
 \theta_{N,d}=O_m\!\left(\frac{(\log d)^{7/2}}{d^2}\right)=o(1).
\tag{9}
\]

Thus monotone Weingarten backtracking is not the remaining logarithmic-moment obstruction. The
exact remaining family is the set of positive-genus contraction skeletons already present at
minimal monotone length: nonplanar permutations and index-loop identifications before any extra
Weingarten backtrack is inserted. The previous exponential free-reduction collision count
controls their annular/genus-zero support but not their higher-genus absolute mass.

The correct next target must allow a factor polynomial in `N` per handle, for example

\[
 B_{N,h}\le C_0^N N^{\alpha h},
\tag{10}
\]

rather than demand `B_{N,h}<=C^N` uniformly in `h`. At `N=O(log d)`, the series
`sum_h B_{N,h}d^{-2h}` can still converge with a large margin.

## 2. Exact degree bookkeeping

For

\[
 S_U=\frac1{m^2}\sum_{v\ne a}
 (U_v^*U_a)\otimes(U_a^*U_v),
\tag{11}
\]

the exact even moment is

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(S_U^N)
 =\frac1{m^{2N}}\sum_{\mathbf e\in E_m^N}
 \operatorname{Tr}W_U(\mathbf e)\,
 \overline{\operatorname{Tr}\check W_U(\mathbf e)}.
\tag{12}
\]

Each of the two traces contains `2N` unitary matrix factors. Across their product there are
exactly `2N` unstarred and `2N` starred factors. Haar phase invariance makes a term vanish unless
for every label `v` the number of `U_v` factors equals the number of `U_v^*` factors. Denote this
common number by `p_v`. This proves (1).

For every active label, the standard unitary Weingarten formula introduces two permutations
`sigma_v,tau_v in S_{p_v}` and the factor

\[
 \operatorname{Wg}_d(\pi_v),
 \qquad
 \pi_v=\sigma_v\tau_v^{-1}.
\tag{13}
\]

The Kronecker constraints generated by `sigma_v,tau_v`, together with the two trace cycles,
determine the contraction skeleton and its number of free matrix-index loops. Expansion (2) then
decorates that fixed skeleton by monotone transposition paths.

This separation is useful:

- changing `g` in (2) does not change the Kronecker contraction skeleton;
- it multiplies the same skeleton by a further power `d^{-2g}` and a positive path count;
- the sign `(-1)^{ell(pi_v)}` is independent of `g`.

Thus excess monotone paths can be summed skeleton by skeleton without taking a factorial union
over all paths.

## 3. Monotone-path theorem and exact interface

The primary results used here are Theorem 2.7 (the exact parity-refined series), Lemma 2.8
(monotone-factorization identification), and Theorems 3.1 and 3.2 (coefficient and summed bounds)
of Collins--Matsumoto, *Weingarten calculus via orthogonality relations: new applications*,
arXiv:1701.04493.

Their path graph has:

- a solid step `sigma -> (i,p)sigma` at the current level `p`;
- a dashed step deleting a fixed last point; and
- exactly `p` dashed steps before reaching the empty permutation.

A path contributing to `Wg_d(pi)` has `ell(pi)+2g` solid steps. Equivalently it is a monotone
factorization into transpositions `(s_j,t_j)` whose larger entries `t_j` are weakly decreasing.
This gives (2), and their combinatorial estimate is exactly (3). In particular, when

\[
 d>\sqrt6\,p^{7/4},
\tag{14}
\]

the complete series obeys

\[
 1\le
 \frac{(-1)^{\ell(\pi)}d^{p+\ell(\pi)}
       \operatorname{Wg}_d(\pi)}
      {P_0(\pi)}
 \le
 \frac1{1-6p^{7/2}/d^2}.
\tag{15}
\]

Here

\[
 P_0(\pi)=|\operatorname{Moeb}(\pi)|
\tag{16}
\]

is a product of Catalan numbers over the cycles of `pi`. The theorem is uniform far beyond the
needed logarithmic regime because `(log d)^{7/2}/d^2 ->0`.

## 4. A repeated-label obstruction to a \(C^N\)-per-genus bound

The lower bound in (3) already refutes a genus coefficient bounded by `C^N` uniformly in `g`, but
the obstruction can be seen explicitly.

Take `pi=id_p`. At level `p`, for every `i in {1,...,p-1}`, the two consecutive solid moves

\[
 id_p\longrightarrow(i,p)\longrightarrow id_p
\tag{17}
\]

form a length-two backtracking excursion. Concatenate `g` such excursions, choosing `i`
independently each time, and then take the `p` dashed deletion steps. This gives at least

\[
 (p-1)^g
\tag{18}
\]

distinct monotone paths contributing to `P_g(id_p)`.

Now choose the Werner edge sequence

\[
 e_1=\cdots=e_N=(1,2).
\tag{19}
\]

Then

\[
 W_U(\mathbf e)=(U_1^*U_2)^N
 =\check W_U(\mathbf e),
\tag{20}
\]

and the integrand is

\[
 |\operatorname{Tr}(U_1^*U_2)^N|^2.
\tag{21}
\]

Both labels have balanced degree

\[
 p_1=p_2=N.
\tag{22}
\]

Choose the identity Weingarten permutation for label `1`, insert the `g` excursions (17), and
keep label `2` at minimal path length. The corresponding absolute diagram coefficient contains
the factor `(N-1)^g`. Therefore, for every proposed universal `C`, choosing

\[
 g>\frac{N\log C}{\log(N-1)}
\tag{23}
\]

makes this one repeated-label coefficient larger than `C^N`.

This is an obstruction to the literal coefficientwise goal, not to the moment itself. All these
terms carry the compensating factor `d^{-2g}`; signs and other contraction skeletons may also
cancel in the complete integral. No lower bound on `E Tr(S_U^N)` or on `||S_U||` follows.
Indeed, `H=U_1^*U_2` is itself Haar, so for `N<=d`

\[
 \mathbb E|\operatorname{Tr}H^N|^2=N
\tag{23a}
\]

exactly. Thus the superexponential positive coefficients isolated above cancel across the other
contraction skeletons for this complete word integral. The example diagnoses the failure of an
absolute coefficientwise proof especially clearly; it is not a bad moment family.

## 5. Product bound for all tuple labels

Fix one balanced edge sequence and one contraction skeleton
`(sigma_v,tau_v)_v`. Put

\[
 M_v:=P_0(\pi_v),\qquad
 a_v:=6p_v^{7/2}.
\tag{24}
\]

If the total excess genus is `g=sum_v g_v`, equation (3) gives

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\sum_{\substack{(g_v):\,\sum_vg_v=g}}
 \prod_vP_{g_v}(\pi_v)\\
 &\qquad\le
 \left(\prod_vM_v\right)
 \sum_{\substack{(g_v):\,\sum_vg_v=g}}
 \prod_va_v^{g_v}\\
 &\qquad\le
 \left(\prod_vM_v\right)
 \left(\sum_va_v\right)^g.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{25}
\]

The last inequality uses the multinomial expansion, whose coefficients are at least one.
Convexity and (1) imply

\[
 \sum_va_v
 =6\sum_vp_v^{7/2}
 \le6\left(\sum_vp_v\right)^{7/2}
 =6(2N)^{7/2}.
\tag{26}
\]

Equations (25)--(26) prove (6). Summing `g>=1` after restoring `d^{-2g}` proves (8).
This estimate is uniform in the number of active labels, the multiplicity profile, all
permutations, the edge sequence, and `m`.

## 6. Skeletonwise remainder reduction

Let `D` denote a complete contraction skeleton: an edge sequence, all
`(sigma_v,tau_v)`, and the resulting Kronecker index identifications. Strip every Weingarten
factor down to its minimal monotone coefficient `P_0(pi_v)`. Let `b_0(D)` be the absolute value
of that minimal-path term, including all powers of `d`, and let `b_+(D)` be the sum of absolute
values of the same skeleton with at least one positive excess `g_v`.

Equations (7)--(8) give the finite statement

\[
 b_+(D)\le
 \frac{\theta_{N,d}}{1-\theta_{N,d}}\,b_0(D).
\tag{27}
\]

Summing over any collection `mathfrak D` of skeletons preserves it:

\[
 \sum_{D\in\mathfrak D}b_+(D)
 \le
 \frac{\theta_{N,d}}{1-\theta_{N,d}}
 \sum_{D\in\mathfrak D}b_0(D).
\tag{28}
\]

This is the strongest completed finite-`d` conclusion of this artifact. It eliminates all
arbitrarily long monotone backtracking tails from the frontier. The only remaining absolute sum
is the set of minimal-path skeletons.

For `N<=c_m log d`, eventually `theta_{N,d}<=1/2`, and

\[
 \frac{\theta_{N,d}}{1-\theta_{N,d}}
 \le\frac{12(2N)^{7/2}}{d^2}.
\tag{29}
\]

Thus a purely exponential minimal-skeleton absolute mass `C^N` would make the monotone-path
excess negligible at logarithmic order. By contrast, a factorial-scale
`exp(O(N log N))` bound is still unusable: at `N=c_m log d` it becomes
`d^{O(c_m log log d)}`, which eventually defeats `d^{-2}` for every fixed `c_m>0`. The
skeleton-entropy problem cannot be hidden inside (29).

## 7. What the previous collision count does and does not supply

`WERNER_REVERSAL_COLLISION_ENCODING_20260830.md` proves that the forward/reverse cyclic collision
edge sequences number at most

\[
 (2N+1)81^Nm^{N+2}.
\tag{30}
\]

At fixed `N`, second-order freeness controls the signed sum of annular minimal-path diagrams. This
is enough for the fixed-order leading covariance. It is not automatically a bound on

\[
 \sum_D b_0(D),
\tag{31}
\]

because (31) takes absolute values before summing the permutation skeletons. Two unresolved
effects remain:

1. minimal-path permutations can already create positive topological genus through their index
   loop deficit; and
2. the number of such nonplanar skeletons is factorial under a completely unrestricted
   permutation count.

Equation (28) shows that improving the monotone Weingarten coefficient itself cannot resolve
these effects: the hard entropy sits in the contraction skeletons, not in the inverse-Gram tail.

## 8. Correct next transfer bound

Let `mathfrak D_{N,h}` be the minimal-path contraction skeletons with topological loop deficit
`2h` relative to their annular or disk maximum, after the return sector is separated. A
sufficient replacement for the false coefficientwise target (4) is

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{D\in\mathfrak D_{N,h}}b_0(D)
 \le
 m^2\left(\frac{C_0}{m}\right)^N
 \left(\frac{C_1N^\alpha}{d^2}\right)^h.}
\tag{32}
\]

The placement of `d^{-2h}` may equivalently be included in the definition of `b_0`; what matters
is an exponential planar baseline and a factor `N^{O(h)}` rather than `N^{O(N)}` for a fixed
handle number.

Indeed, if `N<=c_m log d`, then

\[
 \sum_{h\ge1}\left(\frac{C_1N^\alpha}{d^2}\right)^h
 =O_m\left(\frac{(\log d)^\alpha}{d^2}\right).
\tag{33}
\]

Combining (28), (30), and (32) would give the needed uniform finite-`d` remainder. The natural
proof object is a paired-stack transfer state consisting of:

- the two free-reduction stacks from the forward and block-reversed words;
- the current boundary permutation of open matrix-index strands; and
- a handle counter incremented whenever a new contraction crosses the planar stack order.

For fixed `h`, one must show that all but `O(h)` steps are forced/noncrossing, giving at most
`C_1^N N^{alpha h}` states. This is the smallest remaining lemma.

## 9. Scope and quantifiers

- Equations (2)--(3) are external exact/uniform Weingarten inputs.
- Equations (5), (18), and (23) give a rigorous obstruction to a universal `C^N` coefficient
  bound independent of `g`.
- Equations (25)--(29) are rigorous finite-`d` bounds for the entire monotone-path excess over any
  fixed family of contraction skeletons.
- No bound on the full minimal-skeleton sum (31) is proved.
- Consequently, no estimate for `E Tr(S_U^N)` at `N=c_m log d`, no high-probability norm bound,
  and no generic Haar counterexample is claimed.
- The already established absolute-unbounded extensive violation remains separate. This note
  concerns only the still-open full-size same-tuple Werner spectral gate.
