## Finding HCP-PHA-IR-01 -- PASS: Collins--Youn interface

Collins--Youn Theorem 3.3, PDF pp. 11--12, works in the left regular representation of a direct
product of free groups. In the source notation, after restricting each factor's infinite generator
set to its first `N` generators, every scalar matrix `a` on `E^k` with ordinary trace zero obeys

```text
|| sum_(v,w) a_(v,w) (U_v)^* U_w ||
 <= N^(k/2) sqrt((1+9/N)^k-1) ||a||_2.
```

Setting `N=m` and `k=J` gives exactly the node's constant and index set. Restriction of the left
regular representation of `F_infinity^J` to the subgroup generated by those letters is a direct
sum of copies of the regular representation of `F_m^J`, so the operator norm is the required
regular-representation norm. The source uses the Hilbert--Schmidt norm
`||a||_2=(Tr(a^*a))^(1/2)` and imposes the load-bearing ordinary trace-zero condition.

**Verdict:** PASS. The node assumption exposes the exact coefficient space, representation,
constant, and norms used by the proof.

## Finding HCP-PHA-IR-02 -- PASS: Bordenave--Collins interface

Bordenave--Collins Section 9.1, PDF pp. 60--61, defines strong convergence in probability as norm
convergence for every fixed noncommutative star-polynomial and records the matrix-linearization
interface. Equation (69), PDF p. 62, embeds independent Haar matrices on separate tensor legs:

```text
V_(i,j) = I^(tensor(j-1)) tensor U_(i,j) tensor I^(tensor(J-j)).
```

Theorem 9.2, PDF p. 63, states that for fixed generator count `m>=2` and fixed leg count `J>=1`,
this tuple converges strongly in probability to the left-regular generator tuple of `F_m^J` as the
per-leg dimension tends to infinity. The atomic theorem has `m>e^18`, so the theorem's `m>=2`
condition is automatic.

Each operator `W_v^*W_w` is a fixed star-word in this tuple once `m,J` are fixed. A finite scalar
linear combination of such words is therefore a fixed scalar star-polynomial. The source's stronger
matrix-valued formulation is compatible but not needed for the proof.

**Verdict:** PASS. The node assumption correctly keeps `m`, `J`, the polynomial, matrix size, and
coefficients fixed before `D -> infinity`.

## Finding HCP-PHA-IR-03 -- PASS: finite-net all-coefficient event

Write `q=m^J` and define on traceless `M_q(C)`

```text
T_D(a)   = sum_(v,w) a_(v,w) W_v^*W_w,
T_inf(a) = sum_(v,w) a_(v,w) u_v^*u_w.
```

For both maps,

```text
| ||T(a)||-||T(b)|| | <= sum_(v,w)|a_(v,w)-b_(v,w)| <= q||a-b||_2.
```

The last constant is `sqrt(q^2)=q`. For fixed `m,J`, the traceless Hilbert--Schmidt unit sphere is
finite dimensional and compact. Fixing a sufficiently fine finite net before sampling, applying
strong convergence in probability to each of its finitely many fixed polynomials, and taking a
finite union of failure events gives one event of probability tending to one. The Lipschitz bound
extends the estimate from the net to every traceless coefficient matrix:

```text
||T_D(a)|| <= (1+eta)m^(J/2)sqrt((1+9/m)^J-1)||a||_2.
```

The potentially enormous net affects only an unquantified finite threshold in `D`. It does not
invalidate the limit because `m,J,eta` and the net are all fixed first.

**Verdict:** PASS. The event is simultaneous in `a`, so it may later be applied to a random,
input-dependent coefficient matrix. No uncountable union of probability events is taken.

## Finding HCP-PHA-IR-04 -- PASS: complementary-channel and global MOE bound

The product channel has `q=m^J` Kraus operators `q^(-1/2)W_v`, so a complementary channel is

```text
L^c(S)_(v,w)=q^(-1)Tr(W_v S W_w^*).
```

Every diagonal entry is `1/q`. For `X=L^c(S)-I_q/q`, `X` is Hermitian and traceless. With the
standard Hilbert--Schmidt pairing (or with transposed coefficient indices under the alternate
convention),

```text
||X||_2^2
 = Tr(S (L^c)^*(X))
 <= q^(-1)(1+eta)K_(m,J)||X||_2,
```

where `K_(m,J)=m^(J/2)sqrt((1+9/m)^J-1)`. The zero case is immediate; division otherwise gives
the uniform purity bound

```text
Tr(L^c(S)^2)
 <= q^(-1)[1+(1+eta)^2((1+9/m)^J-1)].
```

Von Neumann entropy dominates Renyi-2 entropy. Entropy is concave, so the minimum output entropy
of `L` is attained on a pure input; for each pure input, a channel and its complement have the
same nonzero output spectrum. Therefore the displayed simultaneous all-input complement bound is
a genuine global lower bound on `S_min(L)`, rather than a selected-input estimate. Entrywise
conjugation maps input states bijectively and preserves output spectra, hence
`S_min(bar(L))=S_min(L)` deterministically and no second random event is required.

**Verdict:** PASS.

## Finding HCP-PHA-IR-05 -- PASS: conjugate Bell upper bound

For one layer, apply `Phi tensor bar(Phi)` to a normalized maximally entangled input. The
`m^2` Kraus-label outcomes form a pure-state ensemble with initial weights `1/m^2`. The `m`
equal-index labels all produce the same Bell vector because

```text
(U tensor conjugate(U))|Omega> = |Omega>.
```

Merging those labels gives a realizing ensemble with one probability `1/m` and `m^2-m`
probabilities `1/m^2`. The von Neumann entropy of a pure-state mixture is at most the Shannon
entropy of any realizing ensemble, whether the remaining vectors are orthogonal, collide, or
overlap. Its entropy is exactly

```text
2log_2(m)-log_2(m)/m.
```

Using the tensor product of the `J` layerwise Bell inputs makes the joint output the tensor product
of the layerwise outputs, so the upper bounds add and give the artifact's factor `J`.

**Verdict:** PASS. The upper bound is specific to the conjugate partner and matched product Bell
input, exactly as claimed.

## Finding HCP-PHA-IR-06 -- PASS: constant, probability, and absolute-unbounded quantifiers

Let `A=(1+9/m)^J`. At `eta=0`, the logarithmic correction in the side-entropy bound is exactly
`log_2 A=J log_2(1+9/m)`. Continuity permits a fixed positive `eta` making the correction at most
that value plus any prescribed fixed `epsilon`. Combining both side bounds with the Bell bound gives

```text
Delta_(J,D) >= J c_m - 2epsilon
```

on the single simultaneous coefficient event.

For `m>e^18`,

```text
log_2(m)/m > 18/(m ln 2)
                 > 2log_2(1+9/m),
```

where the second strict inequality follows from `ln(1+x)<x`; hence `c_m>0`. With fixed
`epsilon=Jc_m/4`, the event implies `Delta_(J,D)>=Jc_m/2`, and its probability tends to one.

Given `G>0`, choose the fixed integer `J>2G/c_m` before taking the limit. Then the same event
implies the strict bound `Delta_(J,D)>G`. This proves an unbounded sequence of absolute finite-
dimensional MOE pair defects in the order `J` then `D`; it does not prove a uniform rate in `D`,
a usable threshold, or a simultaneous statement over all depths.

**Verdict:** PASS.
