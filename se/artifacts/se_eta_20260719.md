## 1. Type and subgroup audit (Rule 7(a))

Let the query point be (1\in A), and let

\[
 G=S_{(A\sqcup B)\setminus\{1\}}\cong S_{q-1}
\]

be the query stabilizer. The Young branching projectors
(Q_0,Q_z\in\operatorname{End}(V_\lambda)) are the central isotypic
projectors for the (G)-irreducibles (V_{\tau_0},V_{\tau_z}) in
(operatorname{Res}^{S_q}_{G}V_\lambda). They have ranks
(f^{\tau_0},f^{\tau_z}).

For the fixed set (L\subseteq A^\circ), (|L|=j), define

\[
 K_L=S_{(A\sqcup B)\setminus L},\qquad
 \Pi_L={1\over(q-j)!}\sum_{k\in K_L}\rho_\lambda(k).
\]

Thus (Pi_L\in\operatorname{End}(V_\lambda)) is an orthogonal projector
of rank (f^\theta). The completion projector is

\[
 P_{\rm comp}={1\over n!}\sum_{b\in S_B}\rho_\lambda(b)
 \in\operatorname{End}(V_\lambda).
\]

The subgroup containments and commutations are:

1. Since (L\subseteq A^\circ), (S_B\le K_L). Hence
   
   \[
   P_{\rm comp}\Pi_L=\Pi_LP_{\rm comp}=\Pi_L.
   \tag{1.1}
   \]

2. Since (S_B\le G) and every (Q_\gamma) is central in the
   (G)-action,
   
   \[
   [P_{\rm comp},Q_\gamma]=0.                              \tag{1.2}
   \]

3. The tempting containment (K_L\le G) is false. The query point is not
   in (L), so (K_L) moves it; for example, (K_L) contains a
   transposition of (1) with a point of (B), whereas (G) fixes (1).
   Therefore ([\Pi_L,Q_\gamma]=0) cannot be asserted. The literal matrix
   gates below give a rank-two nonzero commutator for every tested branch.

Using only the valid statements (1.1)--(1.2), cyclicity of trace gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \eta_\gamma
 &=\operatorname{Tr}(P_{\rm comp}Q_\gamma\Pi_LQ_\gamma P_{\rm comp})\\
 &=\operatorname{Tr}(Q_\gamma P_{\rm comp}\Pi_LQ_\gamma)\\
 &=\operatorname{Tr}(Q_\gamma\Pi_LQ_\gamma)
  =\operatorname{Tr}(Q_\gamma\Pi_L).                       \tag{1.3}
\end{aligned}
\]

This is a trace overlap of two generally noncommuting projectors. It is not
the dimension of an intersection of invariant spaces: for example,
(eta_0=24/25) at the first gate. This corrects the proposed
"multiplicity-weighted dimension count" interpretation.

Equation (1.3) also proves that (eta_\gamma) is independent of
((a,b,\alpha,\beta,c)). The split enters only through the proposed cap
(B_\theta(q)f^\alpha f^\beta).

## 2. Derivation of the closed form

Let

\[
 T_{\neg1}=\sum_{\substack{D\subseteq(A\sqcup B)\setminus\{1\}\\|D|=j}}
 \Pi_D.
\]

The query stabilizer (G) acts transitively on its (N=\binom{q-1}{j})
(j)-subsets. It conjugates (Pi_D) to (Pi_{gD}), while each
(Q_\gamma) is (G)-central. Therefore

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(Q_\gamma\Pi_D)
 ={1\over N}\operatorname{Tr}(Q_\gamma T_{\neg1})          \tag{2.1}
\]

for every (D) not containing the query.

The independently reviewed one-copy frame calculation gives, on the Young
branches,

\[
 T_{\neg1}Q_0=B_\theta(q)Q_0,qquad
 T_{\neg1}Q_z={B_\theta(q)\over\delta_z+1}Q_z.              \tag{2.2}
\]

This is `se_frames2_review_20260719.md`, (R.18)--(R.20), equivalently its
(R.1)/(R.32). Taking the branch traces in (2.1), then using (1.3), proves
(0.1). No LR multiplicity or color split occurs.

Young branching is multiplicity free and the complete child list is

\[
 \operatorname{Res}^{S_q}_{S_{q-1}}V_\lambda
 =V_{\tau_0}\oplus\bigoplus_{z\in\operatorname{Rem}(\theta)}V_{\tau_z}.
 \tag{2.3}
\]

Hence

\[
 f^\lambda=f^{\tau_0}+\sum_{z\in\operatorname{Rem}(\theta)}f^{\tau_z},
 \tag{2.4}
\]

which turns (0.1) into (0.2). Here each (f^\tau) is the standard-tableau
count (f^{\tau/\varnothing}), computable by the hook formula. Thus (0.1)
is the requested closed tableau formula.

The proposed inequalities are exactly equivalent to the following pure
dimension inequalities:

\[
 \text{(2.8-long)}\quad
 f^{\tau_0}\le Nf^\alpha f^\beta,                           \tag{2.5}
\]

\[
 \text{(2.8-tail)}\quad
 \sum_z{f^{\tau_z}\over\delta_z+1}
 \le {Nf^\alpha f^\beta\over\delta_{\min}+1},              \tag{2.6}
\]

\[
 \text{(2.9)}\quad
 f^\lambda\le Nf^\alpha f^\beta.                           \tag{2.7}
\]

Positive LR coefficient does not imply any of (2.5)--(2.7).
