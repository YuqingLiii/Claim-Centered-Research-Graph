## 2. The physical legal branch maps

### 2.1 The legal block chain and cancellation

BR's row space is the orthogonal sum over matchings.  With
\(Q_h=|h\rangle\langle h|\otimes I\), the legal-row projector is

\[
 P_{\rm legal}=\sum_{h\in S_n}|h\rangle\langle h|
                 \otimes P_{{\rm inj},h}.
 \tag{F3.6}
\]

The alphabet central projection, completion, and strict projection are,
on this row sum,

\[
 I_{S_n}\otimes P_\lambda,\qquad
 I_{S_n}\otimes P_{\rm comp},\qquad
 I_{S_n}\otimes P_\xi.
 \tag{F3.7}
\]

Each operator in (F3.6)--(F3.7) commutes with every \(Q_h\).  Therefore the
legal restriction, alphabet projection, completion, and strict compression
preserve the matching rows, while the normalized row stack contributes
\(n!^{-1/2}\).  After the alphabet Fourier projection, the input of the
strict block is exactly \(\mathcal C\), dimension \(dc\), and its completed
output in each row is contained in \(\mathcal O\), dimension \(R\).

The signed cancellation used here is the reviewed two-step argument at
`se_cancel_identity_review_20260718.md:85-102,104-160`:

1. Peter--Weyl matching of alphabet and position shapes, together with the
   full-position lower bound and equivariance, pins the legal restriction
   \(B_k=\mathcal R(\bar W_k)\) to defect \(k\) on both legal sides.
2. Apply the same legal restriction to
   \(\bar W_k=\bar X_k+\bar Y_k+\bar Z_k+T_k\), then compress both sides to
   defect \(k-1\).  The pinned \(\bar W_k,\bar X_k,\bar Y_k\) terms vanish,
   leaving

   \[
   C_{k\to k-1}=-(Z_k)_{k\to k-1}.
   \tag{F3.8}
   \]

For this note \(k=j+1\).  Equation (F3.8) is an equality between the legal
defect-\(j\) NO and YES spaces; after (F3.7), it is an equality of maps with
effective domain dimension \(dc\).  The minus sign disappears in the Gram.

### 2.3 A termwise reflection identity

Let

\[
 V_\lambda\downarrow S_{q-1}
 =Q_0V_\lambda\oplus\bigoplus_zQ_zV_\lambda,
 \quad
 \tau_0=(q-j-1,\theta),\quad
 \tau_z=(q-j,\theta-z),
 \tag{F3.14}
\]

and put \(p_0=f^{\tau_0}/f^\lambda\),
\(\omega=(1-p_0)/p_0\).  Strictness gives

\[
 Q_0P_\xi=P_\xi,
 \qquad Q_zP_\xi=0,
 \tag{F3.15}
\]

as \(F\times F\) identities whose common effective domain is the full
\(dc\)-dimensional carrier.

For every \(j\)-set \(D\) not containing \(1\), one has

\[
 \boxed{
 Q_0\Pi_DQ_0={q\over q-j}p_0\,\Pi_{D\cup\{1\}}Q_0.}
 \tag{F3.16}
\]

To prove (F3.16), note first that
\(\operatorname{Ran}\Pi_D\subseteq\operatorname{Ran}\Pi_{D\cup\{1\}}\).
The projector \(Q_0\) commutes with the pointwise stabilizer of
\(D\cup\{1\}\).  Hence \(Q_0\Pi_DQ_0\) is supported on the
\(S_{q-j-1}\)-fixed subspace of \(V_{\tau_0}\), which is one copy of
\(V_\theta\), of dimension \(f^\theta\).  It is scalar there by the
\(S_D\)-action.  All \(D\)'s not containing \(1\) are conjugate under
\(S_{q-1}\), and the confirmed long-branch frame identity gives

\[
 \binom{q-1}{j}\operatorname{Tr}(Q_0\Pi_DQ_0)
 =B_\theta(q)f^{\tau_0},
 \quad B_\theta(q)={\binom qj f^\theta\over f^\lambda}.
 \tag{F3.17}
\]

Dividing (F3.17) by \(\binom{q-1}{j}f^\theta\) gives the scalar
\((q/(q-j))p_0\), proving (F3.16).

Since every \(\tau_{I,J}\) fixes \(1\), it commutes with all
\(Q_\gamma\).  Equations (F3.15)--(F3.16) therefore turn each summand of
(F3.13) into

\[

 \left(p_0^{-1}Q_0-I\right)\Pi_{D_{I,J}}P_\xi.
 \tag{F3.18}
\]

This step is termwise; it does not replace the colored orbit by a central

## 3. Normalization bridge

The fixed-row counting block is

\[
 B_h^{\rm cnt}=\gamma_{\rm BR}P_{\rm comp}\widehat b_hP_\xi:
 \mathcal C\longrightarrow\mathcal O,
 \qquad
 \gamma_{\rm BR}^2={q^{-3n}\over n!}.
 \tag{F3.25}
\]

It is \(R\times dc\).  Define the \(dc\times dc\) positive matrices

\[
 A_{\rm raw}={1\over n!}\sum_h
 P_\xi\widehat b_h^*P_{\rm comp}\widehat b_hP_\xi,
 \qquad
 A_{\rm cnt}={1\over n!}\sum_h(B_h^{\rm cnt})^*B_h^{\rm cnt}.
 \tag{F3.26}
\]

The fixed-row coefficient enters here and only here:

\[
 A_{\rm cnt}=\gamma_{\rm BR}^2A_{\rm raw}
 ={q^{-3n}\over n!}A_{\rm raw}.
 \tag{F3.27}
\]

The normalized legal YES and NO restrictions have squared densities

\[
 d_Y={(q)_n\over q^n}={q!\over n!q^n},
 \qquad d_N={q!\over q^q}={q!\over q^{2n}}.
 \tag{F3.28}
\]

The two probability-\(L^2\) legal embeddings contribute

\[
 c_0^2=(d_Yd_N)^{-1}
 ={q^{3n}\over(q)_nq!}.
 \tag{F3.29}
\]

The normalized matching row in (F3.23) produces the average already
displayed in (F3.26); it does not produce a second average in (F3.27).
Using \((q)_n=q!/n!\), every scalar now resolves:

\[
\begin{aligned}
 C_{\lambda,\xi}^*C_{\lambda,\xi}
 &=c_0^2A_{\rm cnt}\\
 &=(d_Yd_N)^{-1}{q^{-3n}\over n!}A_{\rm raw}\\
 &={q^{3n}\over(q)_nq!}{q^{-3n}\over n!}A_{\rm raw}\\
 &={1\over n!(q)_nq!}A_{\rm raw}\\
 &={1\over(q!)^2}A_{\rm raw}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{F3.30}
\]

This is the promised bridge to the final \(1/(q!)^2\).

For the canonical Gram, the counting NO polar is the full-carrier scalar

\[
 C_{N,{\rm cnt}}=d_Nr_{\rm top}\omega I_{dc},
 \qquad
 r_{\rm top}={q^jf^\theta\over j!f^\lambda}.
 \tag{F3.31}
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathsf G_{\rm can}^{\lambda,\xi}
 &=d_Y^{-1}C_{N,{\rm cnt}}^{-1/2}
   A_{\rm cnt}C_{N,{\rm cnt}}^{-1/2}\\
 &={\gamma_{\rm BR}^2\over d_Yd_Nr_{\rm top}\omega}A_{\rm raw}\\
 &={1\over(q!)^2r_{\rm top}\omega}A_{\rm raw}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{F3.32}
\]

Both inverse square roots in the first line contribute one
\(d_N^{-1/2}\); together they give \(d_N^{-1}\).  Finally,

\[
 {q^j(q-j)!\over q!}={r_{\rm top}\over B_\theta(q)}
 \tag{F3.33}

## 4. The carrier inequalities: exact stopping point

The confirmed one-copy equality is

\[
 E_{{\rm out},0}^*E_{{\rm out},0}
 +\sum_z(\delta_z+1)E_{{\rm out},z}^*E_{{\rm out},z}
 =B_\theta(q)I_F,
 \tag{F3.35}
\]

where

\[
 E_{{\rm out},0}:Q_0V_\lambda\to\mathscr F_j^{\neg1},
 \qquad
 E_{{\rm out},z}:Q_zV_\lambda\to\mathscr F_j^{\neg1}.
 \tag{F3.36}
\]

Their domain dimensions are \(f^{\tau_0}\) and \(f^{\tau_z}\), not those
dimensions times \(c\).

The reviewed raw map is

\[
 U_h:\mathscr R_{a,b}\longrightarrow\mathcal C,
 \qquad \dim\mathscr R_{a,b}=T
 =\binom{n-1}{a}\binom{n-1-a}{b}f^\alpha f^\beta,
 \tag{F3.37}
\]

\[
 U_h\in\mathbb C^{dc\times T},\qquad
 U_h^*U_h\preceq I_T,\qquad \|U_h\|_{\rm HS}^2\le T.
 \tag{F3.38}
\]

The recommended order would work if the legal-kernel construction supplied
maps

\[
 K_{h,\gamma}:\mathscr R_{a,b}\longrightarrow\mathcal O,
 \qquad K_{h,\gamma}\in\mathbb C^{R\times T},
 \tag{F3.39}
\]

such that, on all \(c\) carrier directions,

\[
 \boxed{B_{h,\gamma}=K_{h,\gamma}U_h^*:
 \mathbb C^{dc}\longrightarrow\mathbb C^R,}
 \tag{F3.40}
\]

and such that the two confirmed frame Grams imply the single
\(T\times T\) inequality

\[
 K_{h,0}^*K_{h,0}
 +\sum_z(\delta_z+1)K_{h,z}^*K_{h,z}
 \preceq B_\theta(q)^2I_T.
 \tag{F3.41}
\]

Indeed, (F3.38)--(F3.41) would give, with no multiplicity factor,

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \|B_{h,0}\|_{\rm HS}^2
 &\le B_\theta(q)^2T,\\
 \sum_z\|B_{h,z}\|_{\rm HS}^2
 &\le {B_\theta(q)^2\over\delta_{\min}+1}T.
\end{aligned}
\tag{F3.42}
\]

This is exactly (R.33), now with every map typed.

However, neither (F3.40) nor (F3.41) follows from the current foundations.
The literal maps (F3.19) contain the non-\(H\)-normalizing factors

The obstruction cannot be removed by commuting the strict projector to the

\[
