## 2. Hypermetric slack in sign coordinates

Let `z_v` be the 600 cut-membership bits and put `u_v=(-1)^z_v`.  For any
integer vector `b` with `sum_v b_v=1`, set

\[
m_b(z)=\sum_v b_vz_v.
\]

Since `m_b(z)` is an integer,

\[
h_b(z)=m_b(z)(m_b(z)-1)\geq0.                               \tag{1}
\]

This is the hypermetric inequality, and

\[
h_b(z)=\frac{(b^Tu)^2-1}{4}.                                \tag{2}
\]

Thus `h_b` is tight on a cut exactly when `b^Tu` is `+1` or `-1`.

For the 307200 known sign rows, let `U` be their implicit sign matrix and let

\[
M=U^TU.                                                       \tag{3}
\]

Summing (2) over the known cuts gives the exact criterion

\[
\sum_z h_b(z)=\frac{b^TMb-307200}{4}.                        \tag{4}
\]

Because every summand is nonnegative, an integer hypermetric is tight on all
known cuts if and only if

\[
b^TMb=307200.                                                 \tag{5}
\]

Equation (5) is the exact algebraic filter recommended for pentagonal and
higher-hypermetric discovery.

## 4. Complete triangle census

\[

\[

## 5. Cone-level consequence

Let

\[
H(z)=\sum_\tau \lambda_\tau h_\tau(z),\qquad \lambda_\tau\geq0,
\]

be any nonnegative combination of triangle slacks.  If `H` vanished on every
known cut, then summing over those cuts would give

\[
0=\sum_\tau\lambda_\tau\sum_z h_\tau(z).
\]

Every inner sum is at least 1792 by (9), so every `lambda_tau` must be zero.
Therefore:

> **Triangle-cone no-go.**  The only nonnegative combination of ordinary
> triangle hypermetric slacks that vanishes on all 307200 known `C`-level
> cuts is the zero combination.

This is stronger than the failure of one chosen triangle: it rules out the
entire equality-compatible triangle cone at the root.
