\[

\[
 \boxed{C_s=\sum_{j=0}^{s-2}p(j)},
\tag{1.2}

where `p(j)` is the integer partition number and `p(0)=1`.  Consequently

\[
 C_4=4,\qquad C_5=7,
\tag{1.3}

\[
 C_6=p(0)+p(1)+p(2)+p(3)+p(4)=1+1+2+3+5=12,
\tag{1.4}

whereas `1+binom(5,2)=11`.  Thus `s=6` is the first legal obstruction.

This kills the conjunction (1.1) and, in particular, its proposed exhaustive decomposition into

## 2. Exact family and types

Fix a legal critical-family integer `s>=4` and put

\[
 a=s^2,\qquad d=4s+1,\qquad M=ad,
 \qquad \theta=(M-s-1,s,1).
\tag{2.1}
\]

Let

\[
 K=S_d^a\rtimes S_a=S_d\wr S_a\leq S_M
\tag{2.2}
\]

and let `K_0=S_d^a semidirect S_(a-1)` be the subgroup fixing block zero.  Let
`V_a=S^(a-1,1)` be inflated from the outer `S_a` quotient, and define the rational occurrence
multiplicity space

\[
 Z_s=\operatorname{Hom}_{K}(V_a,S^\theta),
 \qquad C_s=\dim_{\mathbb Q}Z_s.
\tag{2.3}
\]

The only stability inequalities used below are

\[
 a\geq s+1,\qquad a-2\geq s+1,\qquad d\geq s+1.
\tag{2.4}
\]

They hold for (2.1) at every `s>=4`.  Hence every partition of a tail of size at most `s+1`
fits in the available ordinary blocks, and every low-row occupancy fits inside a block.  No
unstated eventual-stability range is used.

Because `K/K_0` is the set of `a` blocks,

\[
 \operatorname{Ind}_{K_0}^{K}{\bf1}
 \cong {\bf1}\oplus V_a.
\tag{2.5}
\]

Frobenius reciprocity therefore gives the typed dimension identity

\[
 C_s=\dim(S^\theta)^{K_0}-\dim(S^\theta)^K.
\tag{2.6}
\]

Both fixed spaces in (2.6) are subspaces of the same rational `S_M` Specht carrier.  There is no
selected-copy or dense-carrier assumption.

## 3. Jacobi--Trudi converts the problem to four orbit counts

For `L` equal to `K` or `K_0`, let `O_L(mu)` denote the number of `L`-orbits on `mu`-tabloids.
Equivalently,

\[
 O_L(\mu)=\dim(M^\mu)^L
 =\left\langle \operatorname{ch}\operatorname{Ind}_{L}^{S_M}{\bf1},h_\mu\right\rangle.
\tag{3.1}
\]

The three-row Jacobi--Trudi determinant is the exact identity

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 s_{(M-s-1,s,1)}={}&
 h_{M-s-1}h_sh_1-h_{M-s-1}h_{s+1}\\
 &-h_{M-s}h_{s-1}h_1+h_{M-s+1}h_{s-1}.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Taking the scalar product in (3.1) gives, for either subgroup,

\[
 \dim(S^\theta)^L=
 O_L(M-s-1,s,1)-O_L(M-s-1,s+1)
 -O_L(M-s,s-1,1)+O_L(M-s+1,s-1).
\tag{3.3}
\]

Thus no James-map rank conjecture or finite interpolation is needed: it remains only to classify
four finite-set orbit types uniformly.

## 4. Full-wreath orbit counts

Write

\[
 P_t=\sum_{j=0}^{t}p(j).
\tag{4.1}
\]

For a two-row tabloid with low row of size `t`, a `K`-orbit is exactly the unordered list of low-row
occupancies of the `a` blocks.  By (2.4), this is an unrestricted partition of `t`, so

\[
 O_K(M-t,t)=p(t).
\tag{4.2}
\]

For a three-row tabloid with low rows of sizes `t` and `1`, the block containing the row-three
singleton is distinguished by that singleton.  If it contains `k` row-two points, the remaining
row-two occupancies form a partition of `t-k`.  Hence

\[
 O_K(M-t-1,t,1)=\sum_{k=0}^{t}p(t-k)=P_t.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Substituting (4.2)--(4.3) in (3.3) yields

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \dim(S^\theta)^K
 &=P_s-p(s+1)-P_{s-1}+p(s-1)\\
 &=p(s-1)+p(s)-p(s+1).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{4.4}
\]

The value in (4.4) happens to be `1` at `s=4,5`, which explains why subtracting a single full
invariant line was consistent at both reviewed finite targets.  It becomes `3` at `s=6`.

## 5. Distinguished-block orbit counts

For a two-row tabloid, choose `k` row-two points in block zero and partition the other `t-k`
occupancies among the indistinguishable blocks.  Therefore

\[
 O_{K_0}(M-t,t)=P_t.
\tag{5.1}
\]

For low rows `(t,1)`, split into two disjoint cases.

1. If the row-three singleton lies in block zero, choosing the row-two occupancy of block zero
   gives `P_t` orbits.
2. Otherwise, block zero and the singleton's block have ordered row-two occupancies `u,v>=0`.
   If the remaining ordinary-block occupancy partition has size `j`, then `u+v=t-j`, giving
   exactly `t-j+1` choices.

Thus

\[
 O_{K_0}(M-t-1,t,1)
 =\sum_{j=0}^{t}(t-j+2)p(j).
\tag{5.2}
\]

Put `Q_t=sum_(j=0)^t (t-j+2)p(j)`.  Equations (3.3), (5.1), and (5.2) give

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \dim(S^\theta)^{K_0}
 &=Q_s-P_{s+1}-Q_{s-1}+P_{s-1}\\
 &=P_s-p(s+1).
 \end{aligned}
\tag{5.3}
\]

Finally, subtracting (4.4) from (5.3) as required by (2.6) gives

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 C_s
 &=P_s-p(s+1)-\bigl(p(s-1)+p(s)-p(s+1)\bigr)\\
 &=P_{s-2}=\sum_{j=0}^{s-2}p(j),
 \end{aligned}
\tag{5.4}
\]

which proves (1.2) for every legal `s>=4`.
