## Decision

**Decision C — the macroscopic orbit domain and exact invariant feature algebra are explicit, but
an ordinary continuum support/cofactor density is a singular and insufficient limit.  The exact
missing object is a stratified boundary-layer Remez/jet equation.**

At `q=5`, every legal histogram consists of two occupied complementary-pair slots.  After fixing
total occupancy `N`, the complete invariant polynomial algebra is generated by four quantities of
weighted degrees `2,2,3,4`, with one degree-six relation.  Its Hilbert series gives

`rank(F_<D)=D^3/48+O(D^2)`.

Thus a target cutoff `D=kappa sqrt N` requires `Theta(N^(3/2))` support degrees of freedom; it does
not converge to a finite-node ODE.

More decisively, push any unit-`l1` exact annihilator to normalized occupancy space.  Every weak
limit annihilates every fixed invariant polynomial, hence is the zero signed measure.  Constant
YES/NO correlation can survive only because the label is discontinuous at the NO boundary and the
positive/negative atoms collide there on shrinking lattice scales.  In the dual picture, the
continuum label cannot be uniformly approximated by continuous polynomials with error below one,
while the ordinary continuum primal has no nonzero annihilating finite measure.  The naive limit
therefore loses strong duality and cannot predict finite correlation.

The decisive normal scale is

`xi=D^2 C_t/N^2=Theta(C_t/N)`.

It is order one exactly when `C_t=Theta(N)`, the Phase-14/16 target tail scale at `q=5`.  Lattice
rounding changes `xi` by order one near the NO face, so rounding is not a perturbative final step;
it is part of the leading equation.  Additional corner strata, including the recurring
`C_t=1` atoms, require discrete jet coordinates not visible even in `xi`.

Consequently the present node is not well-posed enough to select boundary/interior support,
cofactor signs, or constant correlation **before** lattice rounding.  A closed construction would
first need the stratified boundary-layer kernel and its matching/birth conditions described below.

## 1. Exact `q=5` orbit generating function

For one unordered slot `tau=(r,u)`, define

`A_{a,b}(r,u)=((r)_a(u)_b+(r)_b(u)_a)/2`.

Its bivariate exponential generating function is exact:

```text
G_tau(z,w)
 = sum_{a,b>=0} A_{a,b}(r,u) z^a w^b/(a!b!)
 = ((1+z)^r(1+w)^u+(1+z)^u(1+w)^r)/2.      (23.1)
```

For `H={tau_1,tau_2}`, all `rho=1` orbit rows are coefficients of

`G_{tau_1}(z,w)+G_{tau_2}(z,w)`.                         (23.2)

All `rho=2` injection rows are coefficients of the polarization

```text
G_{tau_1}(z_1,w_1)G_{tau_2}(z_2,w_2)
+G_{tau_2}(z_1,w_1)G_{tau_1}(z_2,w_2).                  (23.3)
```

The omitted factors `(N)_d^{-1}(2)_rho^{-1}` are nonzero row scalars and do not affect the
annihilating kernel.  Equations (23.1)-(23.3) are the exact generating function behind the finite
matrices used in phases 14-21.

## 2. Four invariant coordinates and one relation

For slot `i`, put

```text
s_i=r_i+u_i,       p_i=r_i u_i,
s_1+s_2=N.
```

Swapping within either slot fixes `(s_i,p_i)`, and swapping the two slots exchanges the pairs.
After fixing `s_1+s_2=N`, define

```text
X=s_1s_2,                                  degree 2,
Y=p_1+p_2=C_t,                             degree 2,
Z=(s_1-s_2)(p_1-p_2),                      degree 3,
W=p_1p_2.                                  degree 4.        (23.4)
```

They obey the exact relation

`Z^2=(N^2-4X)(Y^2-4W)`.                                  (23.5)

Conversely, the orbit features generate these invariants:

- `A_{1,1}` gives `Y`;
- `A_{2,0}` together with fixed `s_1+s_2` gives `X`;
- `A_{1,2}` gives `s_1p_1+s_2p_2`, hence
  `Z=2(s_1p_1+s_2p_2)-NY`;
- the two-slot product of `A_{1,1}` gives `W`.

Thus the invariant coordinate ring has Hilbert series

```text
H(t)=(1-t^6)/((1-t^2)^2(1-t^3)(1-t^4)).                  (23.6)
```

Its graded dimensions begin

`1,0,2,1,4,2,6,4,9,6,...`,

so cumulative dimensions below `D=2,3,...,9` are

`1,3,4,8,10,16,20,29,...`.

This exactly explains the finite ranks observed through `D=8`, without fitting them.  Near
`t=1`,

`H(t)~1/(8(1-t)^3)`,

and therefore

`R(D):=dim V_<D=D^3/48+O(D^2)`.                          (23.7)

At `D=kappa sqrt N`, a basic circuit has

`R(D)+1=Theta(N^(3/2))`

atoms.  A finite collection of smoothly moving support points is already the wrong continuum
type.

## 3. Scaled orbit domain

Set

```text
sigma_i=s_i/N,
pi_i=p_i/N^2,
x=X/N^2=sigma_1 sigma_2,
y=Y/N^2=pi_1+pi_2,
z=Z/N^3,
w=W/N^4.
```

The compact macroscopic orbit domain is the quotient of

```text
sigma_1,sigma_2>=0,
sigma_1+sigma_2=1,
0<=pi_i<=sigma_i^2/4
```

by slot exchange.  In invariant coordinates it satisfies

`z^2=(1-4x)(y^2-4w)`.                                   (23.8)

The label has a particularly sharp form:

```text
NO  iff pi_1=pi_2=0 iff y=0,
YES iff y>0.                                             (23.9)
```

Macroscopic high-`C_t` interior has `y=Theta(1)`.  But the required finite tail at
`D=Theta(sqrt N)` begins already at

```text
C_t=Theta(D^2)=Theta(N),
y=Theta(1/N)=Theta(1/D^2).                               (23.10)
```

Hence the macroscopic coordinate `y` collapses the decisive tail layer onto the NO face.

## 4. High-degree feature asymptotics

For fixed query degree, falling factorials simply converge to monomials in normalized occupancy.
That approximation is not uniform when the degree grows as `sqrt N`.

Let

`a=alpha D+O(1)`, `b=beta D+O(1)`, `D=kappa sqrt N`,

and consider a slot with `r=Nq`, `u=Nv`, where `q,v>0`.  Stirling expansion gives

```text
log ((Nq)_a/N^a)
 = a log q - a(a-1)/(2Nq)+O(a^3/N^2),                   (23.11)
```

and likewise for `u`.  Consequently a row-normalized slot kernel has WKB phase

```text
Psi_{alpha,beta}(q,v)
 = max{alpha log q+beta log v,
       beta log q+alpha log v},                         (23.12)
```

with an order-one amplitude correction containing

`-kappa^2(alpha^2/q+beta^2/v)/2`

on the selected branch.  Two-slot rows add the corresponding injection phases.

For an exact signed cancellation to survive at leading exponential order, positive and negative
support must satisfy, for every scaled query direction, the tropical phase-balance condition

```text
max_{H in supp(w_+)} Psi_theta(H)
=max_{H in supp(w_-)} Psi_theta(H).                      (23.13)
```

On each exposed phase-tie face, the next-order amplitudes and cofactor weights must cancel as
well.  Equation (23.13) is a necessary continuum support equation.  It is not sufficient: it
controls convex/tropical support functions, not label correlation, boundary layers, or global
Remez residuals.

## 5. Ordinary weak continuum measures are forced to zero

Let `W_N` be any legal unit-`l1` signed orbit measure annihilating all feature rows of degree
`<D_N`, where `D_N -> infinity`.  Push it to the compact normalized orbit quotient and call the
result `mu_N`.  Its total variation is one, so it has weak-* convergent subsequences.

Fix any invariant polynomial `P`.  For all sufficiently large `N`, `deg P<D_N`.  The exact
generating algebra above and the falling-factorial-to-power triangular change of basis give

`int P dmu_N=0`.                                         (23.14)

Invariant polynomials separate points of the compact quotient and contain constants.  By
Stone-Weierstrass they are dense in its continuous functions.  Therefore every weak-* limit
satisfies

`mu=0`.                                                  (23.15)

This does not contradict `||mu_N||_TV=1`: positive and negative atoms can collide, so total
variation is lost in weak convergence.  It does show that there is no nonzero ordinary continuum
cofactor density whose moments encode the finite construction.

The correlation functional escapes (23.15) only because the label (23.9) is discontinuous at
`y=0`.  Thus any constant correlation is carried by a singular boundary collision which ordinary
weak convergence deletes.

## 6. The naive continuum primal/dual pair is ill-posed

At finite `N,D`, the exact primal/dual pair is

```text
maximize  <f,W>
subject to F_<D W=0, ||W||_1=1;

minimize  ||f-P||_infinity over P in V_<D.               (23.16)
```

For a basic optimum with support `H_j`, weights `w_j`, and dual `P`, the KKT/Remez equations are

```text
sum_j w_j phi(H_j)=0                  for every phi in V_<D,
f(H_j)-P(H_j)=epsilon sgn(w_j),
|f(H)-P(H)|<=epsilon                  on every legal orbit.   (23.17)
```

If one replaces the finite orbit grid by the compact continuum domain before taking the high-degree
limit, every polynomial `P` is continuous while YES points approach every relevant NO face.  At a
boundary point `b`, continuity forces

```text
|(-1)-P(b)|<=epsilon,
|(+1)-P(x_n)|<=epsilon,  x_n -> b,
```

and hence `epsilon>=1`.  Meanwhile the continuum primal with annihilation against every polynomial
has only the zero finite measure by (23.15), incompatible with unit total variation.

The naive limiting primal is empty and the naive dual distance is one.  Finite strong duality has
not converged to a meaningful continuum strong-duality pair.  Therefore a macroscopic Remez flow
cannot predict the finite optimal correlation.

## 7. The decisive boundary and corner scales

The Chebyshev/Markov endpoint resolution of degree `D` is `D^{-2}`.  Define the normal coordinate

```text
xi=D^2 y=D^2 C_t/N^2.                                    (23.18)
```

For `D=kappa sqrt N`,

`xi=kappa^2 C_t/N+o(1)`.                                 (23.19)

Thus:

- NO has `xi=0`;
- the required `C_t=Theta(N)` tail has `xi=Theta(1)`;
- balanced interior `C_t=Theta(N^2)` escapes to `xi=Theta(N)`.

Changing an unsplit macroscopic slot `(0,sN)` to `(1,sN-1)` changes `C_t` by `Theta(N)` and hence
changes `xi` by order one.  A one-lattice-unit rounding decision is leading order in this normal
coordinate.

There are also corner atoms such as

`{(0,N-2),(1,1)}` with `C_t=1`.

They have `xi -> 0` and collide with NO even in the boundary-layer coordinate.  Resolving them
requires retaining discrete small-slot occupancies `(r,u)=O(1)` as additional jet indices.  The
finite optimal supports from Phase 16 contain NO atoms, `C_t=1` corner atoms, `C_t=Theta(N)` layer
atoms, and `C_t=Theta(N^2)` bulk atoms simultaneously.  No single continuous scaling contains all
four sectors.

## 8. Formal support-flow equation and why it does not close

Within a fixed feature dimension, suppose support points `X_j(t)` and weights `w_j(t)` move
smoothly.  Differentiating the moment equations gives

```text
sum_j [dot(w_j) phi(X_j)
       +w_j grad(phi)(X_j) · dot(X_j)] =0                (23.20)
```

for every feature `phi`.  Differentiating support equioscillation gives, inside one label stratum,

```text
-dot(P)(X_j)-grad(P)(X_j) · dot(X_j)
 =dot(epsilon) sgn(w_j).                                 (23.21)
```

Equations (23.20)-(23.21), plus global complementarity, are the formal Remez support flow suggested
by Phase 21's stable fixed-`D` continuation.

They do not close in the target regime:

1. `R(D)=Theta(D^3)` grows, so the number of particles diverges.
2. When integer `D` increments, about `Theta(D^2)=Theta(N)` new support degrees of freedom are born
   at once; averaged over `N`, the birth rate is `Theta(sqrt N)` per unit `N`.
3. Weak support densities converge to zero by (23.15).
4. Velocities in the normal coordinate (23.18) depend on unit lattice rounding.
5. The label derivative is a boundary distribution, not zero, when particles enter or leave a
   YES stratum.

A source/birth law and boundary matching conditions are therefore not optional corrections; they
are the missing leading-order equations.

## 9. Exact missing continuum equation

A viable limit must replace an ordinary signed measure by a **stratified boundary-layer generating
functional** with at least these sectors:

```text

normal layer: xi=D^2 y=Theta(1);
NO face:     xi=0 with tangential total coordinate sigma_1;
corners:     discrete O(1) split occupancies coupled to macroscopic fillers.    (23.22)
```

For each scaled query direction `theta`, let `K_theta` be the sector-dependent limit of the exact
falling-factorial kernel (23.1)-(23.3), including the order-one corrections in (23.11).  The missing
primal equation is

```text
sum_sectors <nu_sector,K_theta> =0       for every query direction theta,
sum_sectors ||nu_sector||_TV =1,
correlation(nu)>2/3,
tail(nu)>=c_0.                            (23.23)
```

It must be coupled to a stratified dual profile `Q` satisfying

```text
|f-Q|<=epsilon on every sector,
f-Q=epsilon sgn(nu) on active support,
normal-layer / corner / bulk matching conditions,
birth conditions when D increments.                     (23.24)
```

Equations (23.23)-(23.24), with an explicit `K_theta` on every stratum and exact matching laws, are
the precise missing continuum Remez system.  Neither the exact invariant ring nor the Phase-21
continuation data determines these kernels and matching laws by itself.

Until (23.23)-(23.24) are derived, a continuum support location cannot be rounded to a finite
support with controlled moments: rounding changes the leading normal/corner equations.
