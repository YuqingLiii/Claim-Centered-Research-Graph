1. Typed S_N-average and multivariate factorial Vandermonde identity

Fix a finite alphabet A of size q.  For block j in [m], let N_j be a
nonnegative integer, let

    Omega_j = {n^(j) in N_0^A : sum_(a in A) n^(j)_a = N_j},

and let mu_j:Omega_j -> R be a finite signed occupancy measure.  Its
uniform orbit lift L_j mu_j is a signed measure on A^[N_j]: an occupancy
atom n^(j) is spread uniformly over the (N_j)!/prod_a n^(j)_a! strings
with that occupancy.  Put N=sum_j N_j.  The tensor product of the lifted
measures is a signed measure on the fixed positional product
A^[N_1] x ... x A^[N_m], identified with A^[N].  Averaging it by S_N
gives a signed measure bar(mu) on A^[N].  Because a position permutation
does not change global occupancy, the occupancy pushforward of bar(mu) is
the convolution

    nu(n) = sum_(n^(1)+...+n^(m)=n) prod_j mu_j(n^(j)),

for n in Omega_{N,q}.  Thus the average changes the distribution within a
global occupancy orbit, not its total signed weight.

For alpha in N_0^A, write |alpha|=sum_a alpha_a,
(n)_alpha=prod_a (n_a)_(alpha_a), and
binom(alpha;beta^(1),...,beta^(m))
=prod_a alpha_a!/prod_j beta^(j)_a!.  Coordinatewise falling-factorial
Vandermonde gives the identity of functions on prod_j Omega_j

    (sum_j n^(j))_alpha
      = sum_(beta^(1)+...+beta^(m)=alpha)
          binom(alpha;beta^(1),...,beta^(m))
          prod_j (n^(j))_(beta^(j)).

Pairing with the product signed measure therefore gives

    <nu,(n)_alpha>
      = sum_(beta^(1)+...+beta^(m)=alpha)
          binom(alpha;beta^(1),...,beta^(m))
          prod_j <mu_j,(n^(j))_(beta^(j))>.

Every object is scalar-valued after pairing; beta^(j) has q coordinates
and local total order |beta^(j)|.  If block j annihilates all factorial
rows of local order below d_j, then every summand with |alpha|<sum_j d_j
has some |beta^(j)|<d_j and is zero.  Hence the averaged product
annihilates all global factorial rows below sum_j d_j (m*d0 for equal
bands d0).  Under the uniform S_N lift, a compatible one-hot query
monomial on |alpha| distinct positions has expectation
<nu,(n)_alpha>/(N)_(|alpha|); repetitions reduce by idempotence and
incompatible labels vanish.  Therefore this is exactly query-degree
filtration, with no occupancy-degree/query-degree swap.

2. NO-side axis-orientation coherence and its exact cost

Fix an odd-q involution sigma with r=(q-1)/2 nonfixed two-cycles
c={a_c,b_c}.  In each repeated positional block, an axis atom for pair c
puts its positive pair total entirely at one endpoint.  Define its axis
orientation epsilon_(j,c) in {a_c,b_c} to be that occupied endpoint.
The symmetric axis measure makes epsilon_(j,c) uniform; the product block
construction makes these choices independent over j and c.  (The pair
totals may be random, but positive totals do not affect this orientation
calculation.)

After merging occupancies, global NO requires two conditions.  First,
every block must itself be on its all-axis/NO layer: one interior pair in
one block already occupies both endpoints and forces global YES.  Second,
for each fixed pair c, all m block orientations must agree.  Conditional
on all blocks being axis on c,

    Pr[epsilon_(1,c)=...=epsilon_(m,c)]
      = 2*(1/2)^m = 2^(1-m).

Independence across the r pairs makes the exact coherence cost

    prod_(c=1)^r 2^(1-m) = 2^(-r(m-1)).

The S_N average does not change occupancies or the global YES/NO label, so
it neither removes nor adds this cost.  Endpoint-swap averaging also
preserves the equality event.  Thus, in the repeated symmetric product
family, the global-NO signed mass is the product of the blocks' all-axis
layer masses multiplied by exactly 2^(-r(m-1)); it is not merely bounded
by that factor.  This derivation does not cover coupled orientations,
non-product block laws, or blocks whose axis choices are not independent
and symmetric.
