# L3 critical endpoint: higher-Specht endpoint-selector audit

Date: 2026-07-22  
Canonical scope:
`KSUM.B4b.L3.ALT.hidden-frequency-mask.critical-endpoint.ALT.orbit-intertwiner-adversary.ALT.coupled-uic-address-cycle-kernel`  
Verdict: **FAIL for the endpoint-selector/higher-Specht subfamily defined below; PARTIAL for the
registered coupled node**  
Status discipline: proposer-tier only; no numerical claim, DAG edit, or status edit

## 1. Verdict

There is no growing Specht escape if the outer/address coupling is generated by only one or two
selected collision endpoints and the occurrence-level matrices remain the standard BHK
`J_m,mI_m` blocks.

The reason is a representation-selection rule, not a finite-spectrum guess.  Reparameterizing an
addressed presentation while leaving the physical input fixed is a fiber gauge action of `S_a`.
Normalized incidence compression averages this action.  Consequently an address Specht sector
`S^lambda` survives only if the outer selector module contains `S^lambda`.

For one selected endpoint the selector module is

\[
 \mathbb C^{[a]}
 =\operatorname{Ind}_{S_{a-1}}^{S_a}{\bf1}
 =S^{[a]}\oplus S^{[a-1,1]}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

For two ordered distinct endpoints on the same side it is

\[
 \operatorname{Ind}_{S_{a-2}}^{S_a}{\bf1}
 =S^{[a]}\oplus2S^{[a-1,1]}
  \oplus S^{[a-2,2]}\oplus S^{[a-2,1,1]}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

For an unordered pair the last alternating sector is absent and every remaining multiplicity is
one.  For one endpoint on each of the two sides, the gauge group is
`S_a^(L) x S_a^(R)` and (1.1) applies independently on each side.  Hence **no partition whose
depth grows with `a` or `m` has a nonzero compressed numerator in this subfamily**.  Arbitrary
matrix coefficients and controlled direct sums do not alter this selection rule.

The surviving sectors have a complete point-stabilizer branching system of bounded rank.  A
single endpoint has two local address statuses, an unordered pair has three, an ordered pair has
seven, and a left/right pair has four product statuses.  These status matrices partition the
unmarked matrix.  Thus the exact address-layer ratio is at most seven.  Restoring an arbitrary
operator-valued outer `UIC_a` coefficient and the BHK inner blocks gives

\[
 O\!\left(a^{2/3}{m\over\sqrt{m-1}}\right)
 =O(a^{2/3}\sqrt m)=O(N^{11/18})
\tag{1.3}
\]

on the legal critical subsequence.  The required extra factor is
`m^(1/6)=Theta(a^(1/12))`, so a constant address ratio does not suffice.

A full selected-cycle-length square kernel can certainly have growing Specht Fourier support
*before* incidence compression.  This memo does not deny that fact.  It proves that its
endpoint-selector projection retains only (1.1) or (1.2).  To make a growing `lambda` survive,
the outer transition must itself expose a growing representation, for example a full matching or
cycle word rather than one/two endpoints.  That is outside the subfamily audited here; no such
formula or masked bound is supplied.

## 2. Exact legal subsequence and audit scope

For every integer `k>=2`, take

\[
 a=k^2,\qquad q=4k^2,\qquad d=k,\qquad m=k+1,
 \qquad n=a(d+1)=k^2(k+1).
\tag{2.1}
\]

Then the critical remainder parameters are exactly

\[
 p=(n-a)-ad=0,\qquad u=q-4a+1=1,
\tag{2.2}
\]

and

\[
 N=2n=2am,\qquad m=\Theta(a^{1/2}),
 \qquad a=\Theta(N^{2/3}).
\tag{2.3}
\]

There are no long-exclusion variables.  On each side an address fiber is `S_a`; on both sides it
is `S_a^(L) x S_a^(R)`.

The audited **endpoint-selector subfamily** consists of kernels with the following three
properties.

1. The address coefficient is a formula in the relative address permutation and data generated
   by zero, one, or two selected collision endpoints.  The selector sigma-algebra contains no
   full outer matching, growing tuple, or cycle word.
2. All remaining dependence on the outer YES/NO pair is an arbitrary operator-valued coefficient
   for the outer `UIC_a` problem.  Matrix coefficients and finite or parameter-controlled direct
   sums are allowed.
3. Every occurrence-level block is the standard BHK block: `J_m` for unequal outer values and
   `mI_m` for equal outer values.  No joint modification across address cycles is allowed.

The third condition is important.  A law which changes several occurrence blocks coherently is
the separate multi-block possibility already left open by the coupled-node memo and is not
covered here.

## 3. Fiber gauge action and exact incidence projection

### 3.1 Gauge action

Let `G=S_a` for one side.  Reindexing the `a` source blocks by `g in G` and applying the inverse
reindexing to their hidden address does not change the physical input.  It only changes its
addressed presentation.  Denote this unitary action on the presentation space by `U_t(g)` for
answer class `t in {0,1}`.

On the `p=0,u=1` subsequence every incidence fiber has the same size `C=(a!)^2`.  The normalized
incidence maps from the preceding memo obey

\[
 R_tU_t(g)=R_t,
 \qquad
 R_t=R_tP_t,
 \qquad
 P_t={1\over |G|}\sum_{g\in G}U_t(g).
\tag{3.1}
\]

Here `P_t=R_t^*R_t` on a fixed presentation fiber is the orthogonal projection onto vectors
constant on that fiber.  Therefore every lifted kernel satisfies the exact identity

\[
 R_1\widetilde\Gamma R_0^*
 =R_1P_1\widetilde\Gamma P_0R_0^*.
\tag{3.2}
\]

Equation (3.2), together with the exact query identity in the preceding memo, is the only use of
incidence compression in this audit.  There is no assumption that compression preserves an
arbitrary numerator.

### 3.2 Typed Schur pairing

Let `W_t` be the outer selector module on answer side `t`, and let
`rho_lambda:G -> U(V_lambda)` be the real unitary Specht representation of dimension
`f^lambda`.  Put

\[
 c_{t,\lambda}:=\dim\operatorname{Hom}_G(V_\lambda,W_t).
\tag{3.3}
\]

Choose orthonormal intertwining isometries

\[
 J_{t,\lambda,s}:V_\lambda\longrightarrow W_t,
 \qquad 1\le s\le c_{t,\lambda}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

If `(e_b)_(b=1)^(f^lambda)` is an orthonormal basis of `V_lambda`, the normalized invariant
vectors are

\[
 \Phi_{t,\lambda,s}
 ={1\over\sqrt{f^\lambda}}
 \sum_{b=1}^{f^\lambda}
 J_{t,\lambda,s}e_b\otimes e_b.
\tag{3.5}
\]

They form an orthonormal basis of

\[
 (W_t\otimes V_\lambda)^G
 \cong\operatorname{Hom}_G(V_\lambda,W_t).
\tag{3.6}
\]

Let `B:W_0 -> W_1` be an outer selector coefficient and
`A:V_lambda -> V_lambda` an address matrix coefficient.  Both are finite-dimensional typed
operators.  The exact `lambda`-sector contribution after the two incidence projections is

\[
 \left\langle\Phi_{1,\lambda,s},
 (B\otimes A)\Phi_{0,\lambda,r}\right\rangle
 ={1\over f^\lambda}
 \operatorname{Tr}\!\left(
 J_{1,\lambda,s}^*BJ_{0,\lambda,r}A^{\mathsf T}
 \right).
\tag{3.7}
\]

Thus (3.7) is a `c_(1,lambda) x c_(0,lambda)` matrix.  It is identically zero if either
`c_(t,lambda)=0`.  This is the promised exact Schur pairing for
`R_1 \widetilde\Gamma R_0^*`; the common normalized incidence scalar has already been absorbed by
(3.5), and `u=1` supplies no asymmetric factor.

For reference, the raw relative-address coefficient

\[
 K_{\lambda,A}(\sigma,\tau)
 =\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(A\rho_\lambda(\sigma^{-1}\tau)\right),
 \qquad \sigma,\tau\in S_a,
\tag{3.8}
\]

has the exact Schur norm

\[
 \|K_{\lambda,A}\|
 ={|S_a|\over f^\lambda}\|A\|.
\tag{3.9}
\]

Consequently the normalized coefficient
`(f^lambda/|S_a|)K_(lambda,A)` has norm `||A||`.  Equations (3.7)--(3.9) allow arbitrary rank,
arbitrary matrix coefficients, and direct sums.  Different `lambda` sectors are orthogonal Schur
blocks, so a killed growing sector cannot cancel a surviving marked block.

## 4. Endpoint selector modules: complete decomposition

### 4.1 One point

For a selected endpoint `j in [a]`,

\[
 W_1=\mathbb C\{e_j:j\in[a]\}
 \cong\operatorname{Ind}_{S_{a-1}}^{S_a}{\bf1}
 =S^{[a]}\oplus S^{[a-1,1]}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Both multiplicities are one.  Hence (3.7) kills every higher Specht sector exactly.

### 4.2 Two ordered or unordered points on one side

For ordered distinct pairs `(j,l)`, the pointwise stabilizer is `S_(a-2)`, and two applications of
the branching rule give, for `a>=4`,

\[
 W_{2,\mathrm{ord}}
 =\operatorname{Ind}_{S_{a-2}}^{S_a}{\bf1}
 =S^{[a]}\oplus2S^{[a-1,1]}
  \oplus S^{[a-2,2]}\oplus S^{[a-2,1,1]}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The dimensions add to `a(a-1)`:

\[
 1+2(a-1)+{a(a-3)\over2}
 +{(a-1)(a-2)\over2}=a(a-1).
\tag{4.3}
\]

For an unordered two-set,

\[
 W_{2,\mathrm{unord}}
 =S^{[a]}\oplus S^{[a-1,1]}\oplus S^{[a-2,2]}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Thus ordered pairs have multiplicities `(1,2,1,1)`, while unordered pairs have `(1,1,1)`.
There is still no growing-depth partition.

If equal ordered pairs are also admitted, the module on `[a]^2` is the direct sum of the diagonal
copy (4.1) and the distinct-pair module (4.2).  This changes only the constant multiplicities and
does not add a partition.

### 4.3 One point on each side

The actual unique outer collision has one endpoint on each list.  The gauge group is the product
`G_L x G_R`, not the diagonal copy of `S_a`.  Its selector module is

\[
 W_{LR}
 =\left(S^{[a]}\oplus S^{[a-1,1]}\right)_L
 \boxtimes
 \left(S^{[a]}\oplus S^{[a-1,1]}\right)_R.
\tag{4.5}
\]

Therefore each side separately contains only trivial or hook address symmetry.  Treating the two
endpoints as if they generated an arbitrary same-side high character is a type error.

## 5. Exact point-stabilizer branching blocks

Let `H=S_(a-1)` fix the queried endpoint.  The ordinary branching rule is multiplicity-free.  For
the four possible same-side sectors in (4.2),

\[
\begin{aligned}
 S^{[a]}\downarrow_H
   &=S^{[a-1]},\\
 S^{[a-1,1]}\downarrow_H
   &=S^{[a-1]}\oplus S^{[a-2,1]},\\
 S^{[a-2,2]}\downarrow_H
   &=S^{[a-3,2]}\oplus S^{[a-2,1]},\\
 S^{[a-2,1,1]}\downarrow_H
   &=S^{[a-3,1,1]}\oplus S^{[a-2,1]}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.1}
\]

The relevant dimensions are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
 \nu & f^\nu\\ \hline
 [a] & 1\\
 [a-1,1] & a-1\\
 [a-2,2] & a(a-3)/2\\
 [a-2,1,1] & (a-1)(a-2)/2\\
 [a-1] & 1\\
 [a-2,1] & a-2\\
 [a-3,2] & (a-1)(a-4)/2\\
 [a-3,1,1] & (a-2)(a-3)/2.
\end{array}
\tag{5.2}
\]

The formulas involving `[a-3,2]` are read in the stable range `a>=5`; the finite case `a=4` is
obtained directly from the same branching rule and has no asymptotic role.

For the normalized matrix coefficient in (3.9), let `P_mu` be the orthogonal branching projection
from `V_lambda` onto `V_mu`.  Its aligned `H x H` marked Fourier block is exactly

\[
 {f^\lambda\over a f^\mu}P_\mu A P_\mu,
\tag{5.3}
\]

and hence has norm

\[
 {f^\lambda\over a f^\mu}\|P_\mu A P_\mu\|.
\tag{5.4}
\]

For a rank-one coefficient `A=|w><v|`, (5.4) becomes

\[
 {f^\lambda\over a f^\mu}
 \|P_\mu v\|\,\|P_\mu w\|.
\tag{5.5}
\]

The exact scalar prefactors in the stable range are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 \lambda & \mu & f^\lambda/(a f^\mu)\\ \hline
 [a] & [a-1] & 1/a\\
 [a-1,1] & [a-1] & (a-1)/a\\
 [a-1,1] & [a-2,1] & (a-1)/(a(a-2))\\
 [a-2,2] & [a-3,2] & (a-3)/((a-1)(a-4))\\
 [a-2,2] & [a-2,1] & (a-3)/(2(a-2))\\
 [a-2,1,1] & [a-3,1,1] & (a-1)/(a(a-3))\\
 [a-2,1,1] & [a-2,1] & (a-1)/(2a).
\end{array}
\tag{5.6}
\]

In particular, the endpoint hook vector lies in the `[a-1]` branch and reproduces the exact
retention `(a-1)/a` from the preceding memo.  The two depth-two sectors each have a lower-corner
branch with asymptotic retention `1/2`.

Equation (5.3) also explains why arbitrary matrix coefficients do not evade the complete marked
system.  An off-diagonal branching block `P_mu A P_nu`, `mu!=nu`, contributes zero on the fixed
`H x H` relation, but then lies entirely in the moved-point status.  Diagonal blocks obey (5.3).
Cancellation among several `lambda` sectors can reduce a fixed block, but the complementary moved
status is their difference from the unmarked block.

For completeness, write an unmarked `G`-intertwiner on a selector module as

\[
 K=\bigoplus_{\lambda} I_{f^\lambda}\otimes C_\lambda,
 \qquad
 \|K\|=\max_\lambda\|C_\lambda\|,
\tag{5.7}
\]

where `C_lambda` acts on the selector multiplicity space.  After restriction to `H`, the *same
matrix* has exact blocks

\[
 K\downarrow_H
 =\bigoplus_\mu I_{f^\mu}\otimes
   \left(\bigoplus_{\lambda:\mu\nearrow\lambda}C_\lambda\right).
\tag{5.8}
\]

For the ordered-pair module these multiplicity blocks are

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
 \mu & \text{marked multiplicity operator}\\ \hline
 [a-1] & C_{[a]}\oplus C_{[a-1,1]}\\
 [a-2,1] & C_{[a-1,1]}\oplus C_{[a-2,2]}
                         \oplus C_{[a-2,1,1]}\\
 [a-3,2] & C_{[a-2,2]}\\
 [a-3,1,1] & C_{[a-2,1,1]}.
\end{array}
\tag{5.9}
\]

Here `C_[a-1,1]` is a `2 x 2` matrix because the hook occurs twice in (4.2); all other displayed
`C_lambda` are scalars or whatever fixed outer-ancilla blocks they carry.  Equation (5.8) gives

\[
 \|K\downarrow_H\|=\|K\|.
\tag{5.10}
\]

Thus a growing ratio cannot be manufactured merely by calling the restricted Schur constituents
"marked blocks."  Query masking must select local status pieces, treated next.

## 6. Complete local-status ceiling

### 6.1 One point

For one selected endpoint `j`, relative address pairs split into

\[
 \pi(j)=j
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \pi(j)\ne j.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Let the corresponding entrywise pieces be `K^fix` and `K^mov`.  Their supports are disjoint and

\[
 K=K^{\rm fix}+K^{\rm mov}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Therefore, for arbitrary signs, matrices, and direct sums,

\[
 \|K\|\le2\max\{\|K^{\rm fix}\|,\|K^{\rm mov}\|\}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

The two spherical functions surviving (4.1) are exactly

\[
 \phi_{[a]}(\pi)=1,
 \qquad
 \phi_{[a-1,1]}^{(j)}(\pi)
 ={a{\bf1}[\pi(j)=j]-1\over a-1}.
\tag{6.4}
\]

Thus any one-endpoint formula reduces after incidence projection to a fixed-versus-moved law.
Any cancellation in the fixed status makes the moved status large, and conversely.

If a one-endpoint formula tries to isolate the unique coordinate on the comparison input carrying
the same outer value, it has introduced a second selected point.  It is then typed by (4.2), not
(4.1).

### 6.2 Two points

Two unordered pairs have three relative intersection statuses, of sizes `2,1,0`.  Hence

\[
 \|K\|\le3\max_s\|K_s^\bullet\|.
\tag{6.5}
\]

For ordered distinct pairs there are exactly seven equality patterns:

1. identical pairs;
2. reversed pairs;
3. same first point only;
4. same second point only;
5. first point equals the other second point only;
6. second point equals the other first point only;
7. four distinct points.

They are the seven double cosets of `S_(a-2)` in `S_a`; equivalently, seven is

\[
 1^2+2^2+1^2+1^2,
\tag{6.6}
\]

the sum of squared multiplicities in (4.2).  Their entry supports partition the unmarked matrix,
so

\[
 \boxed{\ \|K\|\le7\max_s\|K_s^\bullet\|.\ }
\tag{6.7}
\]

For one endpoint on each independent side, the product of the two fixed/moved splits has four
statuses and gives the analogous factor four.  Thus seven is a universal constant for every
one/two-endpoint selector convention in this memo.

These inequalities are matrix identities followed by the triangle inequality.  They do not
assume positivity, centrality, rank one, bounded representation dimension, or a scalar outer
coefficient.

### 6.3 Exposure by the actual collision queries

The full marked operator system retains all the statuses above.  This is also visible directly in
the outer `UIC` promise.  A YES outer input has a shared value at one left and one right endpoint;
a NO outer input contains that value at exactly one coordinate total.  Therefore one of the two
YES collision endpoints lies on a side where the value is absent from the entire NO list.  On
that side every address partner of the selected endpoint has an unequal outer value and hence uses
the `J_m` block.  Splitting according to which side is absent produces only two transition
sectors.  If instead the formula names the unique equal-value destination, it is an ordered
two-point selector and is already covered by the seven statuses in (6.7).

The equal-value/covariant transport status uses `mI_m` and is the same-address baseline after the
named transport is conjugated away.  It has address ratio one.  Every complementary status uses
`J_m` and is present in a physical query mask.  Consequently omitting equality edges from
`Delta_i` cannot turn (6.7) into a growing gain: either the coefficient is on the transported
baseline, or a complementary marked status retains at least a `1/7` fraction.

## 7. Central, pointed, and selected-cycle-length laws are different

### 7.1 Central class functions

For a central class function, use normalized central projectors `P_lambda` and write

\[
 K=\sum_{\lambda\vdash a}\alpha_\lambda P_\lambda,
 \qquad
 \|K\|=\max_\lambda|\alpha_\lambda|.
\tag{7.1}
\]

The endpoint incidence projection keeps only the `lambda` listed in (4.1) or (4.2).  A central
character with growing depth is a nonzero square address operator, but its compressed endpoint
numerator is zero.

### 7.2 Pointed matrix coefficients

A pointed coefficient is not central.  In one Specht sector it has the form (3.8), with arbitrary
`A`; covariance is supplied by the selected outer point(s).  Its exact unmarked and fixed marked
norms are (3.9) and (5.4).  Off-diagonal branching coefficients are entirely moved, and diagonal
coefficients satisfy the two/seven-piece ceilings.  Arbitrary matrix coefficients therefore do
not add a growing ratio.

### 7.3 Full selected-cycle-length selectors

Let `ell_j(pi)` be the length of the cycle of `pi` containing the selected point.  A formula

\[
 k_j(\pi)=F(\ell_j(\pi))
\tag{7.2}
\]

is invariant under conjugation by `Stab(j)` but is neither generally central nor merely the hook
coefficient.  As a square convolution kernel on `S_a`, it can have Fourier support on many and
even all partitions.  The endpoint incidence projection, however, is the Schur pairing (3.7).
For one point it replaces (7.2) by its projection onto the span of the two spherical functions
(6.4).  It therefore remembers only `ell_j=1` versus the aggregate `ell_j>1`; all distinctions
among lengths `2,3,...,a` are erased.  For ordered two-point data it projects onto the rank-seven
algebra in (6.6).

This is a projection statement about the compressed rectangular adversary, not a statement about
the original square kernel.  Retaining a growing `lambda` would require a growing outer selector
module in (3.3).

## 8. Restoring outer UIC and inner BHK factors

After (3.7), each surviving Schur multiplicity block is an operator-valued adversary coefficient
for `UIC_a` with at most the constant selector multiplicities in (4.1)--(4.5).  Duplicating an
outer input by such an ancilla does not change its oracle.  General-adversary optimality and the
standard `O(a^(2/3))` claw algorithm therefore imply

\[
 {\|B\|\over\max_r\|B\circ\Delta_r\|}=O(a^{2/3})
\tag{8.1}
\]

for every surviving operator-valued block and every finite/controlled direct sum in this
subfamily.

Every unmasked BHK occurrence block has norm `m`.  At an unequal selected outer coordinate a
physical query changes the corresponding `J_m` block to norm `sqrt(m-1)`.  Thus

\[
 {m\over\sqrt{m-1}}=\Theta(\sqrt m)
\tag{8.2}
\]

is the exact inner contribution.  Combining (6.7), (8.1), and (8.2),

\[
 {\|\Gamma\|\over\max_i\|\Gamma\circ\Delta_i\|}
 \le O\!\left(7a^{2/3}{m\over\sqrt{m-1}}\right)
 =O(a^{2/3}\sqrt m).
\tag{8.3}
\]

On (2.1),

\[
 a^{2/3}\sqrt m=\Theta(N^{11/18}),
 \qquad
 a^{2/3}m^{2/3}=\Theta(N^{2/3}),
\tag{8.4}
\]

and the missing address ratio is

\[
 m^{1/6}=\Theta(a^{1/12})\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{8.5}
\]

The constant ceiling seven is `o(m^(1/6))`.  No formula law in the declared endpoint-selector
subfamily attains the required gain.

## 9. Coverage boundary

The **FAIL** verdict covers:

* the infinite legal `p=0,u=1` critical subsequence (2.1);
* one selected endpoint, two ordered/unordered same-side points, or one endpoint on each side;
* arbitrary `lambda=lambda(a,m)`, arbitrary matrix coefficients, and finite or controlled direct
  sums in the relative address permutation;
* central characters, pointed coefficients, and full selected-cycle-length square kernels after
  their exact endpoint-selector incidence projection;
* arbitrary operator-valued outer `UIC_a` coefficients;
* the standard occurrence-level `J_m,mI_m` BHK blocks.

It does **not** cover:

* an outer selector sigma-algebra carrying a growing Specht representation, such as a full
  outer matching, a growing tuple, or a selected cycle word supplied independently on both
  incidence fibers;
* coefficients that use the full colored YES/NO position-incidence graph rather than relative
  addresses plus one/two endpoints;
* joint changes to several occurrence-level blocks, so that the BHK factor (8.2) no longer
  applies.

The first item is exactly what would be required to make a growing address `lambda` survive
(3.7).  No candidate formula or complete masked bounds for it emerged in this pass, so it is not a
PASS and was not tested silently.

## 12. Bottom line

Higher Specht sectors do not help merely because their diagrams are allowed to grow.  Under the
endpoint-only covariance actually supplied by one or two collision endpoints, normalized
incidence compression performs an exact Schur pairing.  A growing address sector has no matching
outer selector representation and its numerator is zero.  The only surviving sectors are
trivial/hook, plus the two depth-two diagrams for a same-side ordered pair; their complete marked
status algebra has rank at most seven and therefore only a constant unmarked/marked ratio.

With unchanged BHK inner blocks this returns the addressed `N^(11/18)` ceiling and misses the
critical endpoint by `m^(1/6)`.  A viable continuation must increase the representation content of
the outer selector itself or alter several occurrence blocks jointly.  Neither continuation is
proved or disproved here.
